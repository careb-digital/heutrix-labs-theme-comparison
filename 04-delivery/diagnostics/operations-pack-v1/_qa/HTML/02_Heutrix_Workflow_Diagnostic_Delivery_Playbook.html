Workflow Diagnostic Delivery Playbook

End-to-end SOP, controls, quality gates and facilitator guidance

Scope

One defined operational workflow

Contracted workshop

Up to 90 minutes | up to four client participants

Evidence review

Up to five documents or 50 pages in total

Target draft

10 business days after payment and receipt of agreed inputs

Client review

Five business days | one consolidated reasonable correction round

 

Operating principle: Workflow first. Technology second. Understand people, information, decisions, handovers and failure points before recommending software, automation or AI.

 

1. Contracted outcome

Heutrix reviews one defined operational workflow, maps how it operates now, identifies material friction and visibility gaps, and recommends the simplest practical improvement options. The Diagnostic supports an informed next-step decision; it is not an implementation project or assurance review.

Included deliverables

  • A current-state workflow map covering key steps, roles, information, decisions, handovers and material failure points.
  • A practical future-state workflow map showing recommended ownership, controls and material changes.
  • A prioritised opportunity list with rationale, dependencies and high-level implementation options.
  • A findings presentation of up to 60 minutes.
  • A concise written report covering scope, evidence, assumptions, observations, recommendations, dependencies and next steps.

4. Stage 1 - Activate the engagement

Entry gate

[ ]

Client Services Agreement and Workflow Diagnostic Order Form are signed.

[ ]

[ ]

Actions

  1. Create the project ID and approved folder structure.
  2. Assign the Engagement Lead, Analyst, Quality Reviewer and Privacy / Security Reviewer.
  3. Copy the signed contract details into the Engagement Setup sheet; do not re-interpret them.
  4. Send the welcome, document request and workshop scheduling communications.
  5. Open the deliverables checklist and place target dates in the project calendar.

No early start: Do not accept client files, begin mapping or run discovery before contract, payment and data-handling gates are complete.

 

5. Stage 2 - Kickoff and data-handling check

Run a short kickoff with the Client Project Owner before reviewing documents. The goal is to make the engagement safe and workshop-ready, not to repeat the fit call.

[ ]

Read back the exact workflow in scope and the explicit start and end points.

[ ]

Confirm out-of-scope adjacent workflows and any contractual special conditions.

[ ]

Confirm the Client Project Owner has authority to consolidate operational facts and feedback.

[ ]

Confirm workshop attendees, roles and availability; maximum four client participants unless changed in writing.

[ ]

Confirm the approved client-controlled Google Workspace, SharePoint or other expressly approved repository.

[ ]

Confirm the default data class: no Personal Information or sensitive information; redacted, de-identified or synthetic material only.

[ ]

Confirm no production system access, local download or client-data AI use is approved by default.

[ ]

Record retention, return/deletion and access end dates.

[ ]

Confirm the evidence list, document limit and due date.

[ ]

Confirm draft, presentation and review targets, noting dates move if dependencies are late.

 

Stop and escalate: If the client says useful work requires identifiable or sensitive data, production access, local download, offshore handling or AI use, stop. Obtain the relevant signed authorisation or change the method before proceeding.

 

6. Stage 3 - Evidence intake and review

  1. Receive files only through the approved repository. Do not ask the client to email protected material.
  2. Log each item in the Evidence Register with source, date, version, data class, relevance and handling status.
  3. Check the engagement limit of five documents or 50 pages in total. Raise a Change Request before exceeding it.
  4. Screen for identifying or sensitive information before substantive review. Quarantine and escalate any unapproved content.
  5. Extract only the workflow facts needed: trigger, role, activity, information, system, decision, handover, output, exception and observed issue.
  6. Record contradictions and gaps as questions for the workshop; do not resolve them by assumption.
  7. Prepare a one-page hypothesis map and question list for facilitation. Mark both as internal hypotheses, not findings.

7. Stage 4 - Discovery workshop

Workshop outcome

By the end of 90 minutes, the team should have a shared, bounded account of the current workflow, key exceptions and pain points; a clear statement of the future outcome; and a list of evidence gaps and owners.

Minutes

Segment

Facilitator focus

0-10

Open and bound

Purpose, no-sensitive-data reminder, start/end, roles, decision rule and parking lot.

10-35

Walk the normal path

Trigger, steps, roles, systems, information, decisions, handovers and completion.

35-55

Test exceptions

Common variants, rework, missing information, escalation, delay and failure recovery.

55-70

Evidence and impact

Frequency, timing, volume, risk, visibility and corroborating evidence.

70-82

Future outcome and constraints

What must be easier, visible or controlled; non-negotiables; human review.

82-90

Playback and close

Read back the workflow, open questions, evidence requests, owners and next dates.

 

Facilitator prompts

Trigger. What event starts the workflow? Who knows it has started? What information must be present?

Activity. What happens next in practice, not only in the procedure? Who does it and where is it recorded?

Decision. What decision is made, using which rule, by whom and with what evidence?

Handover. How does the next person know they own the action? What happens if they do not respond?

Exception. What is the most common reason the normal path breaks? How is recovery managed?

Visibility. Who needs to know status, priority or age? Where do they look today?

Control. What must remain human-reviewed, restricted, logged or approved?

Evidence. How do we know this occurs? Is there a de-identified example, system field, count or time measure?

Future outcome. What should be reliably true 30 days after a useful change, without presuming a specific tool?

Facilitation rules

  • Map what happens, not what participants think should happen.
  • Use role names and workflow IDs; stop names or case details from entering the artefacts.
  • Ask for examples without collecting unnecessary data.
  • Park adjacent workflows, implementation design and compliance debates for later decisions.
  • Do not let the most senior attendee silently define every step; test with the people who perform the work.
  • Read back disagreements and record them as unresolved until the Client Project Owner confirms the fact.
  • Do not record the meeting unless expressly agreed and lawfully authorised under an approved handling plan.

8. Stage 5 - Current-state analysis

  1. Convert workshop notes into sequential step IDs and one normal-path map.
  2. Add decision branches and material exception paths only when they affect the finding or recommendation.
  3. For every step, record role, input, activity, system/location, output, handover, control and evidence source.
  4. Mark pain points with a unique ID and link them to the affected step rather than floating comments.
  5. Separate observed facts, client claims, estimates, analyst assumptions and recommendations.
  6. Validate the factual current state with the Client Project Owner before treating the map as confirmed.

Root-cause lens

Lens

Diagnostic question

People and ownership

Is responsibility clear, authorised and resilient when a person is absent?

Process

Are steps necessary, sequenced and governed by a clear rule?

Information

Is the required information complete, current, findable and entered once?

Systems

Do tools support the process, or create duplicate entry and fragmented status?

Controls

Are approvals, privacy, human review, monitoring and exception handling proportionate?

Use judgement: The weighted score supports ordering; it does not replace judgement. Record dependencies, data/security gates, change impact and confidence. A high score with an unresolved control issue cannot be treated as ready to implement.

 

Future-state design rules

  • Remove or combine unnecessary steps before automating them.
  • Assign one accountable owner and make the next action visible.
  • Capture information once at the earliest reliable point.
  • Use a single source of truth for status where practical.
  • Make exceptions, ageing and overdue actions visible.
  • Keep human judgement for clinical, participant, employment, legal and material operational decisions.
  • Minimise data and restrict access by role.
  • Prefer configuration of approved existing tools before custom build.
  • Use AI only for an approved assistive use case with defined inputs, output review and accountability.
  • Allow 'no technology change' when ownership, rules or process are the real constraint.

  • Do not turn the readout into free solution design. Record implementation questions for a separate scope.

13. Stage 10 - Acceptance, correction and closeout

  1. Issue the draft through the approved repository and start the five-business-day client review window.
  2. Request one consolidated feedback response from the Client Project Owner, tied to the acceptance criteria.
  3. Classify each item as factual correction, acceptance non-conformity, preference change or new scope.
  4. Complete the one consolidated reasonable correction round. Use a Change Request for new scope or preference changes that require extra work.
  5. Issue the final deliverables and complete Deliverable Review and Acceptance.
  6. Complete Handover and Access Closure, including editable files, known limitations, open actions and access removal.
  7. Return or delete project Client Data within 30 days after acceptance, subject to lawful retention and backup cycle, and record the disposition.
  8. Close the project record. Any implementation proceeds only under a separately signed scope.
