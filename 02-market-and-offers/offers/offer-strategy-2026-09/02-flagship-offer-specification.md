# One-Page Flagship Offer Specification

## Heutrix Workflow Transformation

**Architecture refinement — 8 September 2026:** Read the [integrated architecture](06-integrated-offer-architecture.md) for routing and relationships with Diagnostics and AI Guardrails. This file remains the detailed Transformation scope source. Operating approval gaps remain in the [validation register](05-owner-validation-register.md).

**Core positioning:** Improve one important workflow — and make the improvement work in practice.

**One-line promise**  
In a typical 2–4 week project, once the written scope, access, decision-makers and prerequisites are ready, turn one high-friction operational workflow into a documented, tested working process with clear ownership, visible status and next action, staff guidance, management visibility and a handover your team can maintain.

**Primary buyer**  
An Australian disability-provider owner or operations decision-maker responsible for a recurring intake/referral, reporting, evidence or document workflow. Secondary fit: an allied health practice with the same operating pattern. Fit is based on maturity and problem severity, not a public staff-count threshold.

**Best used when**

- one workflow can be named and bounded;
- multiple people, roles or systems touch it;
- work is lost in inboxes, spreadsheets, memory or repeated status checks;
- the buyer has a workflow owner, relevant staff and a decision-maker; and
- the problem justifies a four- or five-figure implementation.

## Controllable result

One agreed non-clinical workflow is mapped, redesigned, configured or built, tested against written acceptance criteria and handed over with staff/admin guidance, client-controlled access, known limitations and 30-day in-scope defect correction.

No promise is made about compliance, audit or registration outcomes, revenue, clinical outcomes, fixed time savings, adoption outside the agreed users, or third-party service availability.

## Baseline scope

1. Fit and feasibility confirmation.
2. One 60–90-minute workflow workshop.
3. Baseline and agreed success measures or a documented baseline plan.
4. Current-state workflow, ownership and handover map.
5. Bottleneck, dependency, data and risk review.
6. Approved future-state workflow.
7. Written acceptance criteria and test plan.
8. Configuration or build in approved tools.
9. Synthetic or de-identified testing first where practical.
10. User acceptance testing and the refinement rounds stated in the proposal.
11. Staff quick-start guidance and administrator runbook.
12. Training and practical handover.
13. Client-controlled access plus agreed editable/exportable artifacts where the platform permits.
14. Ownership, maintenance, dependency and limitation register.
15. Before/after summary against the agreed baseline, without extrapolated savings.
16. Thirty-day stabilisation and correction of qualifying in-scope defects.

The proposal must state the exact users, roles, systems, integrations, views, measures, forms, automation steps, permissions, review rounds and training coverage included. Anything not written into scope is excluded.

A process-only transformation is valid where that is the appropriate improvement. Implement and test the agreed steps, responsibilities, handovers and operating instructions; configuration or build is included only when justified and written into scope. The public method remains **Diagnose → Triage → Build → Handover**; Diagnose here is a delivery activity, not a compulsory purchase of Heutrix Diagnostics.

For AI-enabled functions, assess the use and necessary information boundaries, human review and escalation before enabling it. Existing suitable client controls may suffice; otherwise resolve the gaps before deployment. A separate AI Guardrails purchase is not automatic. Technical enforcement and monitoring are not implied inclusions.

## Customer commitment

- Provide one accountable workflow owner and one decision-maker.
- Make relevant staff available for the workshop, review checkpoints, testing and training.
- Provide timely access to approved systems, permissions, representative templates, forms, reports and de-identified examples.
- Confirm requirements, future state, test cases and acceptance by the agreed dates.
- Arrange internal privacy, legal, clinical, security, procurement or vendor approvals where required.
- Maintain licences, vendor relationships and client-owned accounts.
- Nominate the administrator who will own the workflow after handover.

**Typical meeting load:** one 60–90-minute workshop, two focused review checkpoints, user testing, training and final acceptance. Additional interviews, workshops, rounds or training require written scope.

## Example 2–4 week timeline

| Period | Heutrix work | Client involvement | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before kickoff | Confirm scope, access, users, decision-maker and non-sensitive starting materials | Provide approvals, access and named participants | Ready to start |
| Week 1 | Baseline, current-state map, bottlenecks, ownership and dependencies | Workshop; validate the map and baseline | Current state approved |
| Week 2 | Future-state design, acceptance criteria and initial configuration/build | First review checkpoint; approve future state and tests | Build direction approved |
| Week 3 | Complete in-scope build/configuration, internal testing and user acceptance | Provide timely questions/decisions and complete user testing | Acceptance findings recorded |
| Week 4, if required | Complete agreed in-scope refinements, documentation, training and handover | Second review; final verification and decision-maker acceptance | Handover complete |
| Following 30 days | Stabilisation review and qualifying in-scope defect correction | Report defects with reproducible evidence; operate the workflow | Stabilisation closes |

The 2–4 week window begins only after the written scope, access, availability and prerequisites are in place. Client delays, third-party platform changes, additional requirements or unresolved information issues move the schedule through written change control.

## Acceptance, change control and stabilisation

- Acceptance is based on the written tests, not an unwritten expectation of general business improvement.
- A qualifying defect is an in-scope function that fails the agreed acceptance criteria under the documented conditions.
- New features, changed processes, new systems or roles, material data repair, added integrations, extra review rounds or third-party changes are not defects.
- Heutrix documents the requested change, effect on scope/timing/price and approval required before work proceeds.
- The 30-day period covers qualifying in-scope defects; enhancements and ongoing support are separate.

The refined architecture proposes explicitly scoped normal, exception, missing-information and permission scenarios where relevant. Where post-launch measurement is too short to show impact, state the limitation and agree a measurement owner, period and review date. These additions must fit approved effort and capacity before contracting; functional acceptance does not guarantee longer-term business improvement.

## Ownership and handover

Production accounts should be client-controlled. At handover, the client receives the agreed access, workflow maps, configuration/source exports where available, test/acceptance record, quick-start guide, administrator runbook, maintenance notes and limitations register. Third-party platform intellectual property, licences and export restrictions still apply.

## Tools boundary

Heutrix Workflow Transformation starts with approved existing systems. Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Airtable, Smartsheet and care-management platforms may be assessed, but no product mention guarantees support or integration. Feasibility, permissions, licensing, security, privacy and data paths must be approved in scope.
