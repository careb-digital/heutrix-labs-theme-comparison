## 1. Position and offer hierarchy

Heutrix helps Australian disability support providers understand operational friction, improve one important non-clinical workflow and establish practical boundaries for workplace AI. Allied health is the secondary launch audience.

**Understand what is worth changing. Make one workflow work better. Put practical AI guardrails in place.**

| Offer | Buyer question | Purchase result |
|---|---|---|
| **Heutrix Diagnostics** | What is worth fixing, and what should we do next? | An evidence-based decision and a usable action brief, including a no-build or no-project recommendation when appropriate. |
| **Heutrix Workflow Transformation** | How do we make this defined workflow work better? | One agreed workflow redesigned, implemented, tested and handed over. **Primary implementation offer.** |
| **Heutrix AI Guardrails** | What AI use can we allow, under which controls, and who is accountable? | A practical AI operating framework with use boundaries, approval and review responsibilities, and staff guidance. |

These are three entry points, not three mandatory stages. Workflow Transformation remains the principal implementation offer. Diagnostics resolves uncertainty; AI Guardrails addresses a distinct governance need. Each engagement must be useful on its own.

The approved product name remains **Heutrix AI Guardrails**. The supplied “Heutrix AI Guard” wording is not adopted as a rename.

Forms, trackers, dashboards, integrations, automations and lightweight applications are possible implementation components. A client buys a workflow result; the components are selected after feasibility and scope assessment. No tool or integration is promised simply because it appears in an example.

## 2. Qualification and routing

Start with a free, 20-minute introductory fit conversation. It establishes relevance, readiness and the appropriate paid next step; it does not include a workflow assessment, solution design or professional advice. Use general, non-sensitive descriptions.

| Situation established during the conversation | Route and condition |
|---|---|
| Recurring operational friction, but cause, priority or feasible direction is uncertain | Diagnostics, bounded to an agreed workflow or tightly related problem area. |
| One workflow, owner, users, desired result, systems and access path are sufficiently clear to define scope and tests | Direct Workflow Transformation. A separate Diagnostic is unnecessary. |
| Known or planned workplace AI use needs common rules, accountable review or an approval process | Direct AI Guardrails. Prior Diagnostics or implementation is unnecessary. |
| One workflow is clear, but a material integration, data or access dependency is unresolved | Resolve it before committing to implementation. Use a bounded Diagnostic if paid investigation is needed. |
| AI is proposed inside a Workflow Transformation | Assess the use case and required controls in scope. Existing adequate client controls can suffice; a separate AI Guardrails purchase is not automatic. Resolve material control gaps before enabling the affected AI function. |
| Several unrelated workflows compete for attention | Agree a limited prioritisation boundary before Diagnostics; do not imply an organisation-wide review. |
| No accountable owner, insufficient value, unsafe access, or a need outside evidenced capability | Defer, recommend internal action, decline, or suggest an appropriate specialist. |

Commercial independence does not remove delivery dependencies. A governance framework is not permission to deploy every AI use; a Diagnostic is not proof that every proposed integration is feasible.

## 3. Heutrix Diagnostics

### Positioning and fit

**Identify what is worth fixing — and define the sensible next step.**

For a disability-provider owner or operations lead who sees recurring chasing, duplication, unclear handovers or missing information, but cannot yet confidently choose an intervention. Relevant starting points include intake/referrals, reporting preparation, and evidence/document workflows.

The problem area must be identifiable, but the client need not know the root cause or prepare a process map. Agree the investigation boundary before paid work begins. Several improvement options within that boundary are appropriate; unrelated departmental reviews require separate scope.

### Result and core deliverables

The client receives a decision-ready findings and action pack. The following is the core definition; detail and investigation coverage are agreed in writing:

1. Problem statement, workflow boundary, objectives and evidence reviewed.
2. Current-state map showing roles, handovers, systems and information movement.
3. Baseline or a practical baseline collection plan, with assumptions and evidence limitations.
4. Ranked friction, likely causes, observed operational risks and dependencies. Distinguish verified observations from hypotheses.
5. Feasible options, including process-only change, better use of existing tools and no-build/no-action where justified.
6. Recommended direction and future-state outline, explaining value, effort, dependencies and uncertainty.
7. Draft success measures and acceptance criteria for any recommended implementation.
8. A next-action brief naming the proposed owner, prerequisites, stop/go conditions and first-project scope where implementation is justified.
9. A decision readout and agreed editable findings/brief formats, subject to the written ownership terms.

A no-build conclusion still delivers a useful decision. Where evidence is insufficient, specify what remains unknown, the information needed and the decision it would resolve; do not disguise uncertainty as a confident diagnosis.

### Delivery and client effort

Define the question; map the actual work; assess causes and options; review the recommendation with the decision-maker; hand over the decision pack. The scope states the workshops/interviews, materials, systems, review rounds and timing included. The client supplies a workflow contact, relevant staff, representative non-sensitive materials and a decision-maker. Diagnostics has no fixed public duration.

### Completion test

The agreed investigation is documented, material assumptions and gaps are visible, options are compared and the client can decide whether to act, investigate further or stop. The readout occurs and the agreed deliverables are handed over. Acceptance does not require purchase of a further Heutrix service.

### Boundary and next step

No production build, configuration, deployment, major migration, extensive data cleansing, enterprise transformation, managed IT or specialist assurance is included. Additional workshops, prototypes or technical experiments must be explicitly assessed and scoped; they are not implied by “Diagnostics”.

The client may implement internally, improve an existing process, use another provider, request Workflow Transformation, address a separate AI governance need, or stop. Reuse approved Diagnostic artefacts in later work and quote only the remaining effort. Preserve the existing **no automatic fee credit** policy.

### Buyer questions

**We already know the problem.** If the workflow, result and dependencies are clear enough to scope and test responsibly, start directly with Workflow Transformation.

**Why pay for assessment?** The purchase is a usable decision pack that helps determine whether and how to invest. It has value even if Heutrix does no implementation.

**Will you always recommend a build?** No. Process changes, existing-tool configuration, further evidence collection and no further action are valid conclusions. Heutrix should explain its implementation interest where relevant; it should not imply complete commercial independence.

## 4. Heutrix Workflow Transformation

### Positioning and fit

**Improve one important workflow — and make the improvement work in practice.**

For an accountable operations decision-maker with one recurring non-clinical workflow that matters enough to fund and fix. The agreed boundary identifies its trigger, end point, users, roles, systems and exclusions. Intake/referrals, reporting preparation and evidence/documents remain the lead workflow families.

### Result and core deliverables

One agreed workflow is documented, redesigned, implemented, tested against written acceptance criteria and handed over with clear ownership, status and next action, staff guidance and client-controlled access. The [flagship specification](02-flagship-offer-specification.md) remains the detailed scope authority.

Core coverage includes the baseline or baseline plan; current and approved future state; written tests; in-scope process changes and any justified configuration/build; user acceptance and agreed refinements; staff guidance and training; administrator runbook; agreed access/exports; maintenance and limitation notes; and a before/after summary limited to available evidence.

Technology is conditional. A process-only improvement can qualify: implement the agreed responsibilities, handovers, instructions and operating controls, then test the workflow. Do not invent a software deliverable to justify the offer.

### Delivery, timing and client effort

Use the existing public method **Diagnose → Triage → Build → Handover**:

1. **Diagnose:** Validate the workflow, baseline and current state. This is an implementation activity; it does not mean a separate Diagnostics purchase.
2. **Triage:** Approve the future state, dependencies, scope, controls and acceptance tests.
3. **Build:** Implement the agreed process and supporting components; test normal, missing-information, exception and permission scenarios relevant to scope; run user acceptance.
4. **Handover:** Complete agreed refinements, train the team, transfer agreed access/documentation and record operating responsibilities.

The current typical window is **2–4 weeks once written scope, access, decision-makers and prerequisites are ready**. It is a conditional planning window, not an unconditional deadline. The written scope defines changes to timing. Keep the existing meeting model: one 60–90-minute workshop, two focused review checkpoints, user testing, training and decision-maker acceptance. Additional effort is stated before purchase.

### Completion, ownership and stabilisation

Completion is judged against written tests under documented conditions. The client receives the agreed artefacts, staff/admin guidance, known limitations and ownership responsibilities. Accounts should be client-controlled; source/configuration exports are subject to agreed IP terms and platform limits.

Retain the **30-day stabilisation path for qualifying in-scope defects**. Do not reduce it to an unspecified “review”. The detailed start point, response targets and contractual wording remain validation items. New features, changed requirements and third-party changes are separate scope decisions.

Where the measurement period is too short to establish impact, report that limitation and hand over a measurement plan with an owner and review date. Acceptance of delivered functions is distinct from longer-term business impact. Do not promise fixed savings, universal adoption, clinical results or compliance outcomes.

### Boundary and next step

Exclude unrelated workflows, core-platform replacement, unassessed integrations, extensive data remediation, ongoing helpdesk/managed IT, indefinite development and professional decision-making. Client participation, licences, approvals and administration remain necessary.

The team operates and maintains the workflow after handover. Further support or another workflow can be separately scoped; neither is an automatic subscription or a fourth public product.

### Buyer questions

**Is this just automation?** It can include removing steps, clarifying ownership, improving information capture and using existing tools more effectively. Automation is one possible component.

**Will we replace our software?** Existing approved systems are assessed first. Feasibility, access, licensing and data paths are confirmed before any connection is promised.

**How much disruption should we expect?** The proposal names the decisions, reviews, testing and training required from your team, plus any agreed rollout arrangements.

## 5. Heutrix AI Guardrails

### Positioning and fit

**Give workplace AI clear boundaries, practical controls and accountable human oversight.**

For leadership that needs to manage known or planned AI use: which uses and tools may be allowed, which information must be kept out, when a person must review the work and who can approve a new use case. The engagement may cover an agreed organisational area or defined use-case set. “Across the organisation” is appropriate only when that coverage is explicitly scoped.

The accountable sponsor is a leader who can approve internal rules and assign an ongoing owner. Relevant operational, technology and governance stakeholders contribute. Do not promise to discover every unreported use of AI.

### Result and proposed core deliverables

The client receives an AI operating framework that connects policy to actual decisions. These requirements refine the existing offer and need delivery validation before becoming a standard contracted package:

1. A scoped inventory of known and intended tools/use cases, recording information involved, purpose, owner and review status.
2. Assessment criteria covering potential consequences, information sensitivity, output reliability, human oversight and dependencies, with limitations recorded.
3. An approved/conditional/prohibited use matrix for the assessed scope. Unassessed tools or uses do not become approved by omission.
4. Practical acceptable-use and information-handling guidance, with human-review requirements and stop/escalation conditions.
5. A repeatable tool/use-case approval process, named decision roles and a register recording decisions and conditions. The client authorises approval and risk acceptance.
6. A reporting and escalation path for inappropriate use or unreliable output, linked to the client's existing processes where suitable.
7. Staff-facing guidance and an agreed briefing covering representative allowed, conditional and prohibited scenarios.
8. A management handover naming the ongoing owner, review schedule/triggers, unresolved actions and framework maintenance responsibility.

Optional extensions may include additional teams/use cases, extra staff sessions or detailed tool assessments. Technical enforcement, tenant configuration, monitoring and software deployment are not included merely because the offer mentions “controls”. Any such work requires separate capability, access and scope assessment.

### Delivery and client effort

Understand known/intended use; assess the defined cases; draft boundaries and approval paths; review decisions with leadership; brief the agreed audience; demonstrate the process and hand over ownership. The client provides known-use information, relevant policies, stakeholder time and an authorised approver. Scope names coverage, workshops, review rounds, briefing format and timing. No fixed public duration is established.

### Proposed completion test

The sponsor has reviewed and approved the in-scope rules; the register and approval path are usable; agreed representative scenarios demonstrate when to allow, condition, stop or escalate a use; staff guidance/briefing is delivered; and an owner accepts the review process and unresolved action list. High-consequence or unresolved cases remain restricted or deferred pending the required assessment.

Completion demonstrates that the agreed framework has been established. It does not establish universal staff adherence, continuous visibility or elimination of AI risk.

### Boundary and next step

This is operational governance support. It does not provide legal, privacy, clinical or regulatory advice, compliance certification, cybersecurity auditing, continuous employee monitoring or a managed security product. Accountable professional judgement remains with the client and its advisers.

AI-enabled workflow development is separately scoped through Workflow Transformation where appropriate. Diagnostics is useful only if the value or workflow remains unclear. Ongoing governance belongs to the client, with review triggered by relevant changes in tools, use, information or incidents. Further Heutrix reviews require separate scope.

### Buyer questions

**We already have a policy.** Test whether people can apply it: who approves a new use, what information is allowed, who reviews output and what happens when something goes wrong. Reuse suitable existing controls.

**Do we need to adopt AI?** No. Restricting or declining a use can be the correct outcome. A ban or permission should reflect the assessed context; do not claim that all bans fail.

**Is this an AI security tool?** No. It is a scoped governance engagement. The name does not imply continuous technical protection or monitoring.

## 6. Shared commercial and evidence rules

No public prices, ranges, pricing page or pricing CTA. Replace the supplied public-facing “PRICING REQUIRES CONFIRMATION” placeholders with:

> Every engagement is fixed in scope and price before paid work begins. The written scope confirms deliverables, client responsibilities, assumptions, exclusions, timing, acceptance criteria, dependencies, third-party costs and change triggers.

Internal pricing, margins, payment and other commercial rules remain subject to [pricing approval](../../pricing/PRICING-POLICY.md). This refinement sets no prices, discounts, retainers, service levels or capability claims.

State what is core, what is optional and what is excluded. Do not use a long “may include” list as a substitute for the purchase definition. Before contracting, validate delivery capacity and write the exact coverage into scope.

Use observed workflow evidence and explain estimates. An illustrative scenario demonstrates the method; it is not client proof. Published case studies must follow the existing permission and evidence records, and must not imply the same results for a new client or evidence of AI Guardrails delivery where that has not been established.

## 7. Illustrative routing example

**Synthetic example, not a client result:** A provider repeatedly chases missing referral information. If the causes and priority are unclear, Diagnostics maps the handovers and compares options. If the owner, workflow and feasible improvement are already defined, Workflow Transformation can implement and test the agreed capture, ownership and status process directly. If staff also want AI to draft follow-up text, assess that use and its information/review controls before enabling it. Organisation-wide AI rules may justify AI Guardrails, but the referral project does not automatically require all three offers.
