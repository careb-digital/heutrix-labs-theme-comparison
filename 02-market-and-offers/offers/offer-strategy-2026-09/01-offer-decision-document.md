## Executive decision

Heutrix Labs should stop presenting six equal service choices. The public offer architecture becomes:

1. **Heutrix Diagnostics** — a paid, standalone decision product when the first workflow or feasible solution is not yet clear.
2. **Heutrix Workflow Transformation** — the primary implementation product for one bounded operational workflow.
3. **Heutrix AI Guardrails** — a practical governance product for responsible workplace AI use.

Dashboards, trackers, forms, handovers, lightweight internal systems and automation become possible deliverables within Heutrix Workflow Transformation rather than separate public products. Regulated-provider workflow tools remain sector/use-case language, not a fourth offer.

The category Heutrix should own is:

> Diagnose the right operational problem, transform one bounded workflow, or put practical AI guardrails in place.

## 1. Primary ideal client

### Primary launch segment

Australian disability support providers are the primary launch segment.

The target buyer is an owner, chief executive, operations lead, general manager or manager accountable for a recurring non-clinical workflow. The organisation is mature enough to have multiple people or roles touching the work, but the operating process still depends on inboxes, spreadsheets, memory, manual status checks or inconsistent handovers.

Fit is defined by maturity rather than public headcount:

- there is recurring workflow volume rather than a one-off inconvenience;
- the work crosses roles, teams or systems;
- one person is accountable for the workflow;
- the failure is visible enough to justify a four- or five-figure implementation;
- relevant staff can join discovery and testing;
- a decision-maker can approve scope and acceptance; and
- the organisation wants a bounded improvement rather than a whole-platform replacement.

### Secondary launch segment

Allied health practices remain a secondary launch segment. The same offer applies where a practice has a recurring intake/referral, reporting, evidence or document workflow with multiple users, unclear ownership or weak management visibility.

Allied health should keep its own landing page and examples, but the homepage should not give it equal strategic weight until Heutrix has comparable proof and a sharper allied-health workflow thesis.

### GP clinics

GP clinics are a future segment, not a launch segment.

Reasons:

- the local website is built for disability providers first and allied health practices second, with no GP launch copy;
- no GP-specific public page or launch path has been approved;
- the project contains no verified GP-specific delivery capability, case proof or supported-system position; and
- generic references to recalls, Medicare, accreditation or clinical systems could create expectations Heutrix has not substantiated.

The local website cleanup is complete. Keep GP references out of future launch metadata and copy. Reconsider the segment only after Heutrix defines a non-clinical GP workflow, supported-system boundary, proof plan and dedicated page.

## 2. Buyer situation

### Priority problems

Lead with three workflow families:

1. **Intake and referrals** — status, ownership, follow-up, missing information and handover.
2. **Reporting preparation** — agreed inputs, completion status, review, overdue items and management visibility.
3. **Evidence and documents** — required items, ownership, expiry or review dates, missing information and retrieval status.

These are examples, not promises that every version of the workflow is feasible or in scope.

### Current-system pattern

The client usually has a core care/practice management system plus some combination of email, spreadsheets, shared drives, forms, chat, manual reminders and reporting exports. The offer does not assume those systems should be replaced.

### Buying triggers

The most credible triggers are:

- the same follow-up failure or missing-document problem keeps recurring;
- growth, a new service or a new team exposes a person-dependent process;
- reporting requires repeated chasing or manual collation;
- a manager cannot see what is waiting, blocked, overdue or ready for review;
- a key staff member is leaving or the workflow is too dependent on one person;
- an existing tool is underused because the process, ownership or definitions are unclear; or
- leadership is ready to fund a bounded fix but not a replatforming programme.

Do not manufacture urgency. The fit call should help the buyer assess the cost and operational consequence of leaving the workflow unchanged.

## 3. Good fit and not a fit

### Good fit

- One operational workflow can be named and bounded.
- The workflow is important enough to fix now.
- A workflow owner and decision-maker are available.
- Relevant staff can explain the real process and participate in testing.
- Current tools, permissions and representative materials can be accessed lawfully and safely.
- The buyer accepts that discovery may show process clarification is more useful than automation.
- The buyer wants client-controlled accounts, documentation and handover.
- The buyer can work within a fixed scope and written change-control process.

### Not a fit

- The buyer wants legal, privacy, clinical, registration, audit or regulatory advice or an outcome guarantee.
- The need is a complete practice, participant, case-management or enterprise platform replacement.
- The project requires Heutrix to make clinical, safety, compliance or other accountable professional decisions.
- No one owns the workflow or can approve scope.
- Staff or system access cannot be provided.
- The buyer expects an integration before feasibility, permissions, vendor limits and data handling have been assessed.
- The problem is too small or occasional to justify paid Heutrix Diagnostics or implementation.
- The project requires enterprise procurement, security, support or multi-site rollout capability that has not been evidenced.
- The buyer wants a generic chatbot, broad AI training or automation for its own sake.

## 4. Controllable outcome

### Public promise

> In a defined 2–4 week engagement, turn one high-friction operational workflow into a documented, tested working process with clear ownership, visible status and next action, staff guidance, management visibility and a handover your team can maintain.

### Acceptance definition

At handover, one agreed non-clinical workflow will have:

- an agreed baseline and success measures;
- a documented current state and approved future state;
- configured or built in-scope functions;
- test evidence against written acceptance criteria;
- guidance for staff and the client administrator;
- client-controlled access and agreed export/configuration artifacts where available;
- known limitations, dependencies and maintenance responsibilities recorded; and
- a 30-day stabilisation path for qualifying in-scope defects.

This is an operating-system outcome, not a promise of compliance, revenue, clinical results, staff adoption, fixed time savings or uninterrupted third-party services.

## 5. Value Equation decisions

| Lever | Decision | How the offer improves it |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Sell control of one important workflow, not “automation” | Name the workflow, the operational failure and the finished state: owner, status, next action, guidance, management view and maintainable handover. |
| Perceived likelihood | Make the delivery logic observable | Baseline, current/future maps, agreed acceptance tests, synthetic or de-identified testing first, user acceptance, documentation, client ownership and 30-day in-scope defect correction. |
| Time delay | Publish a bounded window and early milestones | Bookable 20-minute call, response within two business days and a typical 2–4 week Workflow Transformation window subject to access and approvals. |
| Buyer effort | State the commitment before purchase | One 60–90-minute workshop, two focused review checkpoints, access to the workflow owner and systems, user testing, training and one decision-maker. |

## 6. Diagnostics-to-Transformation policy

Heutrix Diagnostics must be useful if the client never buys implementation.

### Heutrix Diagnostics produces

- a defined problem and workflow boundary;
- a current-state map;
- an agreed baseline or baseline plan;
- ranked bottlenecks, risks and dependencies;
- feasible improvement options, including a no-build option;
- a recommended future state;
- draft success measures and acceptance criteria;
- a decision-ready first-project scope; and
- written no-go conditions.

### Commercial rule

Do not automatically credit or discount the Diagnostics fee. Reuse its approved artefacts so the client does not pay for the same discovery twice; price Workflow Transformation for the remaining design, build, testing, training and stabilisation work.

This makes Heutrix Diagnostics a paid decision product rather than disguised sales work. The client may stop with a useful plan, seek another implementer or proceed with Heutrix. Heutrix should give an honest no-build recommendation when the benefit case, access or feasibility is weak.

### When a client can go directly to Workflow Transformation

Direct Heutrix Workflow Transformation is appropriate when one workflow, owner, desired operating result, users, systems and access path are already sufficiently clear to create a fixed scope and acceptance criteria without a separate diagnostic engagement.

### Independence and dependencies across all three offers

Each offer is separately purchasable. Diagnostics is not a compulsory implementation stage; AI Guardrails can be purchased directly for known or planned workplace AI use. The Diagnose activity within Workflow Transformation is not a separate Diagnostics purchase.

Where implementation includes AI, confirm the use-case controls before enabling the affected function. Suitable existing client controls may suffice. Material gaps must be resolved, but a separate AI Guardrails engagement is not automatically required. AI Guardrails establishes operational governance; technical enforcement, monitoring and AI-enabled workflow builds need explicit capability and scope assessment.

## 7. Public choice architecture

### Starting point 1 — Not sure what to fix

Choose **Heutrix Diagnostics**.

### Starting point 2 — One workflow is defined

Choose **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**. This is the primary implementation card.

### Starting point 3 — Workplace AI needs clear boundaries

Choose **Heutrix AI Guardrails**.

### Product architecture

| Product | Position | Scope rule |
|---|---|---|
| Heutrix Diagnostics | Standalone paid decision deliverable. | Use when the priority, feasibility, baseline or acceptance criteria are not yet clear. |
| Heutrix Workflow Transformation | Primary implementation product. | Automation, dashboards, management visibility and lightweight internal systems are possible mechanisms or scope variants—not separate products. |
| Heutrix AI Guardrails | Workplace AI governance product. | Keep the scope operational: appropriate use, information boundaries, accountable review, escalation and staff guidance. |

Regulated-provider and disability-provider language remains a sector lens and source of relevant use cases, not an additional product.

## 8. Tools position

The public principle is **approved existing tools where feasible; no forced replatforming**.

Relevant products to assess include:

- Microsoft 365: Excel, Forms, Lists, SharePoint, Teams, Power Automate and Power BI;
- Google Workspace: Sheets, Forms and Drive;
- work-management tools such as Airtable or Smartsheet; and
- care-management platforms such as ShiftCare, SupportAbility or Lumary as possible source, reference or destination systems.

Naming a product does not promise that Heutrix supports every feature or offers a native integration. Every system, connection, licence, permission, API/export path and data-handling requirement must be checked before it is included in scope.

Current product references: [Microsoft Lists and Power Automate](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/SharePoint/lists/documents-and-library/create-a-flow-for-a-list-or-library), [Google Forms](https://workspace.google.com/products/forms/), [Airtable](https://support.airtable.com/articles/1747995963-introduction-to-airtable-basics), [Smartsheet](https://www.smartsheet.com/content/workflow-automation-software), [ShiftCare](https://shiftcare.com/solutions/ndis-providers-software), [SupportAbility](https://www.supportability.com.au/) and [Lumary](https://www.lumary.com.au/).

## 9. Commercial position

Do not publish a pricing page, public prices, starting ranges or pricing CTAs at launch.

Use this public wording:

> Every engagement is fixed in scope and price before paid work begins. After the fit call, Heutrix will recommend Heutrix Diagnostics, Heutrix Workflow Transformation, Heutrix AI Guardrails or no project. Scope, dependencies, third-party costs and change triggers are stated in writing.

Internally, continue to use the H4 pricing and margin model. Do not quote below the modelled margin floor without narrowing scope or documenting an approved exception.
