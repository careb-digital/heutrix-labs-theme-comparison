# Target-market fit at Heutrix’s proposed prices

**Date:** 5 September 2026  
**Status:** Internal recommendation for owner review; not an approved change of market, price, scope or public positioning  
**Decision owner:** Janith  
**Evidence cut-off:** 5 September 2026  
**Decision sought:** Narrow the disability-provider prospecting profile and authorise a controlled comparison with selected allied health and accounting firms once outreach gates are ready.

## Recommendation

Heutrix’s current broad audience is insufficiently selective for its proposed economics. The strongest near-term choice is **established disability support providers with funded, high-volume administrative problems**, particularly workforce onboarding, invoice exceptions and management reporting. Keep disability as the launch specialism while testing alternatives. Do not yet replace it with a general small-business or AI consultancy positioning.

The distinction matters: the best launch market is the one in which Heutrix can win and deliver profitable work using its existing credibility. The theoretically wealthiest sector may require substantially more selling, learning and assurance effort. Current evidence favours a narrower disability segment for launch; it does not establish that disability is the best long-term market.

Recommended priorities:

1. **Primary:** financially viable, established disability providers with repeatable non-clinical workflows, an operations or finance owner and a plausible total implementation budget around $20,000–$40,000. These are internal screening amounts, not approved quotes.
2. **Secondary:** allied health groups with central administration, meaningful workflow volume and mixed/private funding. Small practices and heavily NDIS-dependent therapy businesses should not be treated as an automatic affordability upgrade.
3. **Challenger:** established accounting and outsourced-finance firms buying an internal document, exception or workflow improvement. Validate willingness to pay and differentiation from practice software before investing in sector positioning.
4. **Later:** legal firms, insurance brokers and larger commercial service businesses, where economic potential is plausible but Heutrix-specific access, capability and proof are not established.

Confidence is high in the arithmetic, moderate in the recommendation to narrow the current segment, and low-to-moderate in alternative-sector rankings. No completed willingness-to-pay study or repeatable sales conversion record was identified in the reviewed controls and sales records.

### Delivery complexity can overwhelm the segment advantage

The following stress test increases both roles’ hours by 50%, holds non-labour costs unchanged, and retains the model’s contingency and collection allowance at high rates:

| Offer | High-rate planned GM | GM with 50% more labour hours | Implication |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Diagnostics | 65.4% | 50.1% | A broad organisational assessment does not fit the current ten-hour allowance. |
| Standard | 70.6% | 58.4% | Falls below the proposed project floor. |
| Visibility | 71.7% | 60.4% | Barely clears the floor; misses target. |
| AI Guardrails | 65.6% | 50.5% | Extra interviews and review rounds are material. |
| Expanded | 71.9% | 60.9% | A larger fee does not eliminate delivery risk. |
| Support | 80.1% | 72.2% | Misses its distinct 75% target. |

At $16,000, Standard has $889 of direct-cost room above the high-rate estimate before reaching 65% GM, equivalent to about eight additional consultant hours at $100 plus contingency. Diagnostics has only $14 and Guardrails $64 of equivalent room. These are **headroom beyond an estimate that already contains contingency**, not an assertion that no rework is budgeted.

A Standard project with eight extra consultant hours and ten extra developer hours returns approximately 62.0% GM. Replacing the 26 offshore hours with a hypothetical $100/hour domestic developer, with no offshore uplift, produces 60.5% GM. The domestic rate is a sensitivity assumption, not a market quote. Approved synthetic development and controlled deployment may preserve the offshore model; client data restrictions must be resolved before quoting.

Discounting the unchanged Standard scope to $10,000 yields only 53.5% GM at high rates. Its calculated fee for 65% GM is $13,386 and for 60% is $11,670, before additional scope or acquisition effort. These are mathematical boundaries, not recommended discounts. A smaller offer needs genuinely fewer hours and deliverables.

## 2. What a suitable buyer must be able to justify

Heutrix is asking customers to buy an implementation outcome at a material fixed fee. A minor inconvenience or a generic wish to use AI is inadequate.

**Illustrative buyer economics — not a client outcome or market benchmark:**

| First-year cost component | Without ongoing support | With twelve months of support |
|---|---:|---:|
| Diagnostics, if needed | $3,600 | $3,600 |
| Standard implementation | $16,000 | $16,000 |
| Customer staff time allowance | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Incremental software allowance | $2,400 | $2,400 |
| Optional support | $0 | $21,600 |
| Total first-year economic cost | **$25,000** | **$46,600** |

Diagnostics is not mandatory for an already understood, feasible scope and is not automatically credited. Support is not mandatory, and the existing thirty-day in-scope defect period must not be sold twice. Actual software, client effort, contract term and support commencement will vary.

Assume 46 working weeks, $65/hour of relevant customer labour value and a conservative 60% realisation factor. One hour of weekly time opportunity then contributes $1,794 of annual economic value. Covering the cost above within twelve months requires approximately **14 hours/week without support or 26 hours/week with support**. To recover an amount equal to the full first-year cost within six months requires about 28 or 52 weekly hours, respectively. This is a deliberately conservative six-month test: a cash-payback model would time the monthly costs and ramp-up separately. All cases assume the benefit begins promptly; delayed adoption pushes recovery out.

The correct test is customer-specific net benefit: avoidable expenditure plus incremental contribution from genuinely usable capacity, less recurring costs. Do not count released hours as cash savings if salaries remain unchanged and capacity cannot be redeployed. Do not value new appointments or jobs at gross revenue; deduct their variable delivery costs and verify demand. Do not add faster collections as recurring profit: cash release and financing savings are different benefits. Do not double-count onboarding, invoice and reporting savings that describe the same event.

For a $16,000–$19,500 implementation, an initial screening hypothesis is **$40,000–$60,000 of credible annual economic opportunity**, with a realistic path to six-to-twelve-month recovery of total costs. Support and Expanded scopes need their own, usually higher, threshold. This is a qualification recommendation, not a promised result.

A revenue screen alone is weak. Illustratively, a $3 million business at 3% operating margin earns $90,000; a $25,000 first-year change consumes 28% of that amount. A $10 million business at the same margin earns $300,000 and the same investment is 8%. Neither example proves available cash. Ask about funded budget, liquidity and authority, not merely turnover.

## 3. Disability providers: retain the advantage, tighten selection

The [NDS 2025 report](https://wabluesky.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/NDS8221-NDS-State-of-the-Disability-Sector-Report-2025_FINAL-1.pdf) reports that 48% of respondents made a loss in 2024–25; 81% of respondents delivering NDIS supports said they could not continue at current prices. These are survey findings, not the finances of every Australian provider or the exact proposed segment. They show why pain and purchase capacity must be assessed separately.

The [2026–27 NDIA pricing review](https://www.ndis.gov.au/providers/pricing-and-payments/pricing/what-annual-pricing-review-apr) and the government’s [reform consultation information updated 2 September 2026](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/ndis-legislation-changes/engagement-consultation) also make it inappropriate to extrapolate an old provider budget unchanged. Check each prospect’s exposure to pricing, service and funding changes. Do not sell unverified regulatory interpretations or assume reform automatically creates consulting budget.

Heutrix’s strongest counterweight is its [existing evidence map](../../06-marketing/case-studies-and-assets/SOURCE-EVIDENCE-MAP.md): owner-confirmed delivery across four Australian disability providers covering onboarding, invoicing, reporting and incident workflows. The fifteen case studies span four providers, not fifteen customers. Selected approximate outcomes have client-feedback support; public permission remains open. This supports domain familiarity, but not repeatable margins or market-wide willingness to pay.

**Recommended initial internal sourcing band:** roughly 30–150 active workers or contractors and approximately $3 million–$15 million annual revenue. These are unvalidated prospecting hypotheses, not market-derived breakpoints, public eligibility rules or interchangeable measures of size. A smaller provider with concentrated, expensive administration can qualify; a larger one with no funded sponsor can fail.

Prioritise organisations with a dedicated operations/finance lead, recurring transaction volume, a service footprint that creates handoffs, an already functioning core system, and one bounded exception workflow. Private ownership may shorten approval, but funded, empowered not-for-profits can also fit. Ownership type and registration are not proxies for financial health.

Best initial workflow hypotheses:

- Worker/contractor onboarding coordination and access handoffs, with legal, employment and screening decisions retained by the customer and advisers.
- Invoice exceptions, reconciliation preparation and management visibility, with human financial authorisation.
- Recurring operational reporting where usable source data already exists.

Deprioritise clinical notes, incident interpretation, automated claims decisions, safeguarding decisions and whole-system replacement as first offers. These introduce more sensitive data, professional dependencies or critical-service risks than the proposed delivery allowances comfortably demonstrate.

Avoid start-ups without stable volume, cash-distressed buyers seeking a rescue, no-budget sole traders, and enterprise tenders whose assurance burden consumes the project economics. A national group can still buy one bounded pilot; its enterprise-wide programme should be separately costed.

### The existing-software challenge

[ShiftCare’s pricing and feature page](https://shiftcare.com/pricing) lists rostering, onboarding, accounting/payroll connections, invoicing and visibility functions. Its Australian-labelled monthly plans shown during this review were $9, $15 and $25 per staff member, excluding GST. At fifty staff this is $450–$1,250/month. Vendor prices and capabilities require reconfirmation for a particular account; these claims are not Heutrix integration credentials.

Software licences and implementation are different purchases, but buyers will compare them. First check whether existing settings, a vendor feature or a focused training session solves the problem. Heutrix’s defensible work is the remaining gap across teams, systems, exceptions, acceptance and adoption. Rebuilding a commodity feature undermines both value and repeatability.

## 4. Alternative markets and segments

The assessments below are analytical judgements, not observed Heutrix sales results. All require direct validation; none inherits proven delivery economics from the disability example automatically.

| Segment | Proposed-fee fit | Advantages | Principal economic obstacle | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Broad small disability providers | Low/variable | Strong need; owner access | Low volume, limited budget, existing-software alternatives | Stop treating the whole audience as equally attractive |
| Established disability providers meeting the screens above | Moderate–high, conditional | Closest proof, recurring administration, recognisable buyer | Funding pressure, sensitive information, hidden exceptions | **First priority** |
| Allied health groups, roughly 10–40 clinicians with central admin | Moderate–high, conditional | Repeat referrals, intake and reporting across locations | Funding mix, clinical boundaries, mature practice systems | **Secondary test** |
| Solo/small allied health practices | Low for current full scope | Accessible owner | Insufficient recoverable administrative value | Refer to software/configuration or assess a separately scoped product |
| Accounting/outsourced-finance firms, roughly 15–60 staff | Potentially high | Recurring document chasing, fixed-fee work, measurable capacity | Crowded practice-software market, confidentiality, partner approval | **Best initial non-health challenger** |
| Legal firms, roughly 15–75 staff | Potentially high | Expensive professional time, intake and matter administration | Trust, privilege, professional risk and multiple approvers | Later, preferably through a trusted introduction |
| Commercial maintenance/facilities firms, roughly 20–100 field staff | Moderate–high for unresolved office handoffs | Quote-to-job-to-invoice gaps; non-clinical workflow potential | Job-management incumbents, onsite work, service-critical integrations | Reserve challenger if owner access is materially better |
| Insurance brokers and other regulated professional firms | Potentially high | Renewals, documents and multiple internal handoffs | Additional domain learning, regulated decisions and security review | Later partner-led exploration |
| Aged care, GP groups and large enterprises | Budget may exist | Large operating footprint | New proof, system and procurement demands | Do not broaden launch into these now |

The alternative staff ranges are research hypotheses for list building, not approved ICP rules. Prefer one challenger with strong access over shallow activity across every industry.

### Allied health: narrow by operating model and funding mix

The [AHPA digital-transformation survey released February 2025](https://www.ahpa.com.au/news-updates/new-survey-report-reveals-allied-health-sector-ready-for-digital-transformation-and-urges-further-policy-and-funding-commitmentnbsp) included 2,419 practitioners across 36 professions. Its focus was digital-health awareness and readiness, not budgets for Heutrix-style implementation. It supports heterogeneity, not a claim of universal purchase intent. AHPA’s [April 2026 response on disability funding](https://www.ahpa.com.au/news-updates/you-cannot-fix-allied-health-in-aged-care-by-cutting-disability-funding) also cautions against assuming that moving from support providers to therapy providers removes funding exposure.

Prefer multiple locations or teams sharing intake and administration, measurable referral delays, and spare clinical capacity or an avoidable admin cost. A full appointment book with no additional staff capacity does not turn faster intake into incremental revenue.

[Cliniko’s current pricing page](https://www.cliniko.com/pricing/) provides tiered subscriptions with unlimited admin users and locations. The page rendered in USD during this review, so no AUD comparison has been inferred. Heutrix must demonstrate an unmet cross-system or organisational problem beyond the practice platform. The reviewed Heutrix proof remains disability-specific; allied health examples must remain labelled synthetic unless separately evidenced.

### Accounting: commercially plausible, competitively demanding

The [Ignition 2025 survey](https://www.ignitionapp.com/au/2025-tax-compliance-pricing-benchmark) covered 165 Australian accounting firms; 80% planned fee increases and 64% cited rising costs as the main reason. This demonstrates pricing discretion and cost pressure, not available transformation budgets. Ignition itself offers proposals, payments and workflow functions, illustrating the substitute problem.

Target operational bottlenecks in fixed-fee recurring work: missing-information chasing, exception routing, workflow ownership and management reporting. Keep tax/accounting judgement and client-money decisions out of scope. A partner or operations manager should validate that released capacity can reduce overtime, defer hiring or support profitable additional work. If staff are underutilised, the economic argument is weaker.

A referral from an accountant into disability clients could also improve acquisition economics within the current market. A referral channel is a route to a segment, not evidence that the accountant should become the primary buyer. Any commission or white-label arrangement needs separate costing, responsibility and data-access terms.

### Legal and commercial services: options, not an immediate pivot

[Macquarie’s 2024 legal benchmark](https://www.macquarie.com.au/assets/bfs/documents/business-banking/bb-legal-industry/legal-benchmarking-report-2024.pdf) reported an average 25% profit margin and 80% of firms above 10%. Crucially, this was a September 2023 survey of 185 firms and calculated profit before partner salaries and dividends; it is neither a 2026 budget study nor comparable with Heutrix’s gross margin. It supports an affordability hypothesis that must be tested against current firm accounts, access and a non-legal administrative scope.

Commercial maintenance may offer a less clinically sensitive workflow, but [ServiceM8](https://www.servicem8.com/au/pricing) already supplies job management, quoting, invoicing, forms and accounting connections. A generic scheduling build is not an attractive thesis. Test a documented gap between office roles or systems, and reprice work requiring site visits or unfamiliar integrations. No sector-wide profitability premium for this segment was established in this review.

## 5. Options for the business model

| Option | Effect on pricing and margins | When to choose it | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Narrow disability, preserve full-scope price hypotheses | Retains proof advantage and current unit economics; rejects low-value opportunities earlier | Enough funded, bounded problems can be reached | **Recommended starting position** |
| B. Elevate mixed-funded allied health groups | Potentially stronger payer mix with related workflows | Direct tests show better conversion and actual contribution | Worth testing; insufficient evidence for an immediate swap |
| C. Pivot to accounting or another business-service niche | Potentially more discretionary spend; new learning and acquisition costs | Challenger repeatedly wins and delivers better economics | Conditional, not justified by sector wealth alone |
| D. Serve smaller buyers through a genuinely smaller offer | Lower fee only works with lower effort, fewer dependencies and low selling cost | Repeated demand for the same small outcome | Design separately after evidence; do not discount the full offer |
| E. Move immediately to enterprise transformation | Higher contract values but more procurement, assurance and staffing exposure | Funded sponsor, capability and delivery capacity are proven | Poor default launch choice |

Keep Workflow Transformation central. Use Diagnostics where ambiguity warrants a paid decision product; do not impose a $3,600 toll on a clearly scoped implementation. Guardrails should follow a concrete workplace-AI need and an accountable sponsor, not generic fear or a promise of compliance. At $10,500 its 28 consultant hours need tight boundaries and practical outputs, not generic policy templates.

The $1,800 support assumption deserves its own validation. It models four delivery hours per month, not unlimited support. Annual support of $21,600 exceeds the Standard build fee. Buyers must understand the actual monitoring, maintenance and response commitment they are purchasing. Where ongoing operational value is low, offer a scoped handover and separately priced change work. Do not quietly convert defect correction into recurring revenue.

The proposed 65% project target is internally coherent with the overhead and rework needs, but it is not proved achievable merely because a model clears it. If qualified buyers reject prices, investigate problem value, proof, scope and competition before changing the floor. Any approved reduction must be tested through finance, sales, delivery and contract implications together.

## 6. Validation plan and decision rules

**Owner:** Janith; delivery and finance reviewers to be named. **Start:** after the applicable outreach gates in the [launch board](../../00-control/LAUNCH-BOARD.md) close. This evaluation does not authorise sending messages or beginning paid work before existing commercial and onboarding gates are satisfied.

### First eight weeks after readiness

Allocate approximately 60% of research/sales effort to the narrowed disability profile, 25% to selected allied health groups and 15% to accounting as a challenger. These are proposed test allocations, not a revenue forecast. Use comparable workflows and record warm versus cold introductions so channel quality is not mistaken for sector fit.

Aim for eight substantive buyer conversations per segment. Ask for workflow frequency, roles, exception volume, time/cost baseline, existing software capability, sponsor, available budget, procurement steps, system/data access and an intended decision date. Do not request identifiable operational records for qualification. Log no-fit reasons, including no response, no budget, weak value, vendor solution, missing trust and infeasible scope.

Discuss the relevant provisional fee privately in the context of a specific scope; issue actual quotes only after pricing approval. Interest in free advice is not willingness to pay. Route ambiguous cases to Diagnostics and clear cases directly to scoped implementation. Seek two paid implementations in the primary segment and at least one comparable challenger engagement where fit exists; if these cannot close within the window, report the evidence as incomplete rather than count verbal interest as a sale.

Track consultant and developer time separately through discovery, configuration, testing, documentation, training, project management and stabilisation. Record acquisition hours, quote preparation, contractor costs, licences, change requests, collection days, defect effort and measurable customer acceptance. For an eight-week decision where stabilisation is unfinished, retain its cost allowance and schedule a review after completion.

### Acceptance criteria for retaining the segment

- At least two independently won, completed comparable projects demonstrate roughly 65% realised gross margin after actual direct costs and a reasonable outstanding-defect reserve; no ordinary scope repeatedly requires the 60% floor exception.
- Buyers accept a credible total-cost/value case and a funded scope; payment and acceptance follow the agreed terms.
- The next prospect can reuse the method and approved components without reusing client data or treating a new workflow as identical.
- There is a plausible route to enough funded opportunities. The current ten annual Transformation projects would require forty qualified opportunities at an illustrative 25% win rate. At a 20% named-account-to-qualified-opportunity rate this means 200 accounts; at 10% qualification and 20% winning it means 500. These are funnel sensitivities, not market-size estimates or outreach-volume commitments.

Two projects are an early operating gate, not statistical proof. If only one project completes, retain uncertainty explicitly. Avoid interpreting the full NDIS provider count as the addressable market: provider type, budget, relevant workflow, reachability and approval capability all reduce it.

### When to promote a challenger or change the offer

Promote a challenger only after at least two comparable completed wins demonstrate customer value, sustainable delivery margin and better acquisition-adjusted contribution than the primary cohort. Use a proposed 20% advantage in contribution per founder hour as a practical decision threshold, with no material deterioration in collection or delivery risk; it is a management rule to approve, not a market statistic.

Calculate contribution after direct delivery cost and attributable acquisition costs, and divide by actual founder time across selling and delivery. Use consistent cost treatment: do not add allocated sales/owner overhead again if already included in the comparison. Maintain the full company P&L separately so unattributed overhead remains visible.

If most well-qualified disability conversations find budgets below $10,000 while their necessary scope remains close to Standard, the current segment/offer combination fails. Choose a genuinely smaller deliverable or a different buyer; do not silently accept 53.5% GM on the same work. If budget exists but engagements repeatedly exceed hours by 50%, fix scope, tooling and delivery before blaming the market. If a vendor configuration solves most problems, consider an approved implementation/channel model with revised economics or leave that use case.

## Verification and evidence limitations

Read the workspace controls, current offer strategy, ideal-client profile, finance guidance, source evidence map and current native workbook. Independently recomputed all six offers’ base direct costs, high-rate margins and high-rate target prices against saved values. Checked cached workbook cells for Excel errors and recalculated the sensitivities in this report. No financial model was changed, so the historical finance audit scripts were not rerun; they audit superseded models. No website, public price, customer communication or approved positioning was changed.

Research used official government, industry-body and direct vendor material available by the evidence cut-off. Survey years and financial definitions differ. Vendor feature lists are substitutes to investigate, not proof of integration capability. No exact addressable-market count, current competing consultancy quote set, buyer interview programme or Heutrix-specific segment win rate was established. Those gaps are why the recommendation is to narrow and test rather than declare a permanent pivot.
