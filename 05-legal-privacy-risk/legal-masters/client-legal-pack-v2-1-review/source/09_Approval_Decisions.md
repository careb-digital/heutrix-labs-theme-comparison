## Legal and source checks

Primary public guidance checked on 13 September 2026 for drafting and issue-spotting. These sources inform the proposed clauses/SOPs; they do not determine Heutrix's legal status or replace Australian legal advice. Verify applicable law and guidance again when the actual operating model is settled.

- ACCC contracts guidance: https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/contracts . Standard-form small-business protections may apply where a party has fewer than 100 employees or annual turnover below AUD 10 million. Review automatic acceptance, termination, indemnities and unilateral rights. A contract may arise through conduct or communications even without a written instrument; the SOP must prevent premature commitments, not merely rely on a signature disclaimer.
- ACCC consumer rights and guarantees: https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees . Check whether consumer guarantees apply to the transaction and preserve non-excludable rights. A business customer is not automatically outside those protections.
- OAIC small-business guidance: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business . Turnover is not the only test; exceptions and specific obligations may apply. Serving a health/disability provider does not alone establish that Heutrix is itself a health service provider. Obtain an applicability assessment based on actual handling and relationships.
- Victorian Health Records Act guidance: https://www.health.vic.gov.au/legislation/health-records-act . Health-information handling in Victoria can attract Health Privacy Principles regardless of business size. Assess jurisdiction and exemptions; do not assume the federal small-business position resolves state obligations.
- OAIC NDB guidance: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/notifiable-data-breaches/preventing-preparing-for-and-responding-to-data-breaches/data-breach-preparation-and-response/part-4-notifiable-data-breach-ndb-scheme . Assess applicability, suspicion/belief thresholds, serious harm, effective remedial action, assessment timing and notification as soon as practicable where required. The 30-calendar-day assessment rule is not a general permission to wait before notifying.
- ACMA spam guidance: https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam . Establish consent, identify the sender and provide unsubscribe. Honour requests within five working days and keep the unsubscribe available for at least 30 days. Consent evidence and suppression handling need an approved operating location.
