# Case-study value and proof assessment

**Status:** Internal editorial review  
**Updated:** 4 September 2026

This assessment applies a simple value principle: make the desired operating result concrete, reduce perceived effort and uncertainty, and prefer specific proof over a larger unsupported promise.

## Collection-level assessment

| Dimension | Current strength | Editorial decision |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity | High | Each story names an actual workflow, tool, handover or control found in the source material. |
| Relevance | High for disability-provider operations | The cases centre on contractor onboarding, invoicing, reconciliation, incident management and shift notes. |
| Proof | Strong for process, workbook, dashboard and incident artefacts; partial for three automation claims | State the source status inside each file and do not flatten all cases into one proof level. |
| Quantification | Intentionally withheld | No publishable baseline, post-change measure, calculation method and permission set was reviewed. |
| Differentiation | High | The work demonstrates workflow-first design across familiar tools rather than selling a single platform. |
| Trust boundary | High | Every case states that the work pre-dated Heutrix and was not a Heutrix client engagement. |
| Buyer effort | Credibly reduced | Each “How Heutrix would deliver this today” section explains the bounded scope and synthetic-test approach. |

## Strongest lead stories

These stories have the clearest combination of operational stakes and direct build evidence:

1. **Power BI invoice reconciliation** — a concrete cross-system management problem with a built report package and detailed comparison model.
2. **Contractor invoice workspace** — an understandable before/after story supported by the process map and two master workbooks.
3. **Invoice discrepancy workflow** — demonstrates that the value sits in the exception path, not merely the dashboard.
4. **Fortnightly data snapshot pipeline** — shows maintainability and repeat operation beyond a one-off visual.
5. **Incident actions and closure** — connects structured information capture to accountable follow-up.

Use these as the first long-form website or sales-deck candidates once permission is resolved.

## Cases that need tighter publication handling

| Case | Why it is compelling | Remaining proof gate |
|---|---|---|
| Pay-cycle reminders | Clear recurring trigger and low-friction next action | Locate the automation export or representative delivery log. |
| Invoice status and payment locking | Strong control story around mutable workbooks | Locate the flow definition and state-transition evidence. |
| Shift-note summarisation | Highly relevant AI-and-workflow example | Review the automation, approved data path, human-review evidence and publication permission. |
| Incident form and register | Shows mature workflow thinking | Historical wording and old review date require current owner and adviser review. |

## Editorial rules retained

- Do not call these “15 client successes”. They are 15 angles from connected founder-delivery work.
- Do not convert the source organisation’s operating data into marketing evidence.
- Do not add a metric because it sounds plausible.
- Do not use “automated” as the outcome; name the handover, exception or decision the automation supported.
- Do not imply that a form, dashboard or workflow established compliance, accuracy or a clinical outcome.
- Keep the strongest useful detail: systems involved, states, checks, views, fields and acceptance boundaries.

The collection is materially more persuasive than a set of generic scenarios because it now shows what was actually mapped or built. Publication remains a separate owner decision.
