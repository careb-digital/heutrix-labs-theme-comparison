# AI-assisted administration kept a named human accountable

> **Illustrative Heutrix application.** This case demonstrates a practical AI-guardrail design for an allied health practice. It is not presented as a client result or as legal, privacy, clinical, compliance or regulatory advice.

## The operational stakes

The practice wants staff to use AI carefully for low-risk administrative tasks: drafting a generic checklist, improving non-sensitive internal guidance or structuring a template for human review.

Leaders also need to stop two predictable failures—sensitive information entering an unsuitable tool and a confident AI draft being mistaken for a professional conclusion.

| Starting point | Proposed operating change | Acceptance signal |
|---|---|---|
| Informal experimentation and broad warnings | Role-based use rules, information boundaries and named review | Staff can identify when to proceed, when review is required and when to stop |

## The real constraint

The boundary is not “AI or no AI”. It changes with the task, information, tool, user and consequence of error.

A generic administrative draft may be appropriate in an approved tool with review. The same tool may be unsuitable for patient information. No tool should replace clinical, safety, legal or other accountable judgement.

## What Heutrix would change

**Heutrix AI Guardrails** would organise the practice’s approved positions into an operating set:

1. A role-based use-case register.
2. Approved, conditional and prohibited categories.
3. Information classification and input rules.
4. A named reviewer and source-check for each approved use.
5. Clear red lines for clinical and other accountable decisions.
6. A short escalation path and staff checklist.

The practice would retain responsibility for tool approval, workforce policy, privacy and security decisions, professional judgement and required advice.

## The promised operating result

Staff would not need to interpret a long policy at the point of use. They would have a short path from task to information rule to tool rule to human review. Managers could approve a narrow use without implying blanket approval of the platform.

The first useful milestone would be agreement on the three to five use cases staff face most often and the non-negotiable red lines.

## How the result would be proved

Synthetic scenarios would test an approved administrative draft, a conditional use, a prohibited patient-information input, an inaccurate output and an uncertain request. Acceptance would require staff to reach the organisation’s approved position and identify the accountable reviewer.

This work would not guarantee correct AI output, adoption or compliance. Guardrails keep human accountability visible; they do not replace it.
