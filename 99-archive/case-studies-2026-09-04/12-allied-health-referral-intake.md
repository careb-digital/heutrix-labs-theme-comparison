# Referral follow-up became a queue, not an inbox search

> **Anonymised founder-delivery experience.** This case draws on intake and allied-health administration workflows handled and improved by members of the Heutrix founding team. Identifying details are withheld. It is not presented as a Heutrix client engagement.

## The operational stakes

Referrals arrived through several channels. Administration checked basic information, authorised professionals made any required suitability or prioritisation decisions, and staff followed up before the next administrative step.

The work was happening, but “under review” could mean three different things. The practice manager could see that a referral existed without being able to see who owned the next action.

| Starting point | Delivered operating change | Observable result |
|---|---|---|
| Referral status reconstructed from inboxes and local notes | One administration queue separating information, professional decision and next action | Staff could see waiting, blocked and ready referrals without chasing colleagues |

## The real constraint

Administrative completeness and professional judgement were mixed together. A referral could wait for missing information, wait for an authorised decision or simply wait for the next person to notice the latest update—all under the same label.

## What changed

The founders’ workflow approach separated the handovers while preserving professional accountability:

- record the referral entry point and initial administrative owner;
- define the minimum administrative completeness categories;
- separate “information needed” from “awaiting authorised decision”;
- record one next action and due date;
- hand over only when the agreed rule is met; and
- close the workflow with an approved outcome category.

Clinical suitability, urgency, allocation and care decisions remained with authorised professionals. The tracking layer did not copy clinical detail that staff did not need for coordination.

## The operating result

Administration gained a workable queue. Professionals could see which items were ready for their decision. Managers could see exceptions without interrupting the team for a status update.

The improvement made the workflow easier to operate without asking the practice to replace its clinical system.

## Proof boundary

This case supports a qualitative claim about referral ownership and visibility. It does not claim increased bookings, shorter wait times, clinical appropriateness or revenue. Those claims require a real baseline and permissioned evidence.
