# Every incident action still had an owner after the meeting ended

> **Illustrative Heutrix application.** This case applies Heutrix’s workflow method to administrative incident follow-up. It is not presented as a client result and does not cover incident assessment, investigation, notification or accountable professional decisions.

## The operational stakes

The immediate incident process may be handled correctly, yet follow-up actions can still disappear afterwards. A procedure needs updating. A document must be obtained. A manager needs to confirm an agreed action. Each item is discussed, but the action trail breaks across meeting notes, email and local task lists.

The desired result is not a new incident system. It is confidence that every authorised follow-up decision becomes a visible action with an owner and closure rule.

| Starting point | Proposed operating change | Acceptance signal |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-up actions split across notes and inboxes | One action register linked to authorised decisions | No action can close without the agreed owner, status and evidence |

## The real constraint

The incident record and the follow-up work are treated as the same thing. One may be closed while operational actions remain open elsewhere. Managers see a list of incidents, but not the dependencies that still require attention.

## What Heutrix would change

The engagement would begin only after the provider defines its approved incident process and accountable roles. Heutrix would then design the administrative action layer:

- create actions only from an authorised decision;
- assign one owner and due date to each action;
- distinguish waiting, blocked, overdue, ready for review and complete;
- require the agreed evidence or review before closure;
- preserve a reason when an action is reopened or changed; and
- give managers an exception view without unnecessary sensitive detail.

Severity, reportability, safeguarding, clinical response, disciplinary action and final incident decisions would remain with authorised client personnel and qualified advisers.

## The promised operating result

Staff would know which action is theirs and what completion means. Leaders would see unresolved follow-up without reconstructing meeting notes. The workflow would carry the decision forward; it would not make the decision.

The first useful milestone would be an approved action and status model. That can expose ownership gaps before any configuration is built.

## How the result would be proved

Synthetic tests would cover a completed action, a blocked dependency, reassignment, reopening, restricted access and attempted closure without evidence. Acceptance would be based on those written tests—not a promise of incident prevention or compliance.

## How Heutrix would scope it

This would be one bounded **Heutrix Workflow Transformation** engagement. Scope would state the roles, information boundary, systems, permissions, views, exceptions and handover artefacts.

Bring the post-decision handover that is hardest to track to a 20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call. No names, incident details or records are needed.
