# Structured shift notes created the input for human-reviewed summaries

> **Anonymised founder-delivery experience.** This story is drawn from operational work completed by Heutrix founders in a prior care-provider role, before Heutrix began formal operations. It is not a Heutrix client engagement. The organisation is withheld pending publication approval.

Summarisation is only as useful as the notes underneath it. When shift information arrives in inconsistent shapes, an automated summary can make the writing shorter without making the record more dependable.

| Starting point | Founder-built intervention | Operating effect |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative notes and routine observations were difficult to review consistently across shifts | Structured digital note capture paired with an owner-attested summarisation automation and human review | Repeated observations could be organised into a concise review draft while responsibility stayed with a person |

## The real constraint

The challenge was not simply “use AI to write a summary”. The workflow had to separate factual entries, routine observations, free-text notes and the accountable interpretation of that information. It also had to recognise that shift notes may contain sensitive information that cannot be moved into an unapproved tool.

## What the founders built

The verified source-capture layer used a digital form and response sheet with fields for shift date and time, behavioural or shift details, routine observations, fluid intake and additional notes. The owner states that the founders also built an automation to summarise notes.

The responsible operating pattern is clear:

1. collect information in a consistent structure;
2. preserve the source notes;
3. generate a summary draft for a defined purpose;
4. require an authorised person to review the draft against the source; and
5. correct, approve or reject the summary before it is relied on.

## The operating result

Structured capture created a more workable input for review and summarisation. The automation could assist with synthesis, but it did not replace the source record or the person accountable for accuracy and escalation.

## Evidence and limits

The digital form-response structure was reviewed without opening or copying completed notes. The owner attests that the summarisation automation was built, but its flow definition, prompt, model, output records, review log and approved data path were not available. This case must not be published as a verified AI outcome until those artefacts, privacy boundaries and permission are reviewed. It makes no clinical, documentation-quality, time-saving or safety claim.

## How Heutrix would deliver this today

This would require both **Workflow Transformation** and **AI Guardrails**. Heutrix would confirm the approved data class, tool, storage, retention, model boundary, reviewer, failure path and source-comparison test. Synthetic notes would be used first; real health or participant information would not enter a new system without the client’s documented authority and controls.
