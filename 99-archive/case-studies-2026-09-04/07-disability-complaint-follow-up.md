# Complaint follow-up stopped disappearing into email

> **Illustrative Heutrix application.** This case shows how Heutrix would improve administrative complaint follow-up. It is not presented as a client result and does not determine findings, responses, remedies or regulatory obligations.

## The operational stakes

A complaint can create several authorised follow-up actions involving administration, operations and management. The original record may be easy to find while the work arising from it is scattered across email threads and personal task lists.

The question that matters is operational: which action is still open, who owns it and what must happen before the next handover?

| Starting point | Proposed operating change | Acceptance signal |
|---|---|---|
| One broad status trying to describe a complaint and every follow-up action | Separate action states, owners, review points and closure evidence | The coordinator can identify every unresolved action without searching email |

## The real constraint

The problem is not a missing complaint form. It is that the case, the accountable decision and the resulting work are not separated clearly.

An internal review date can be mistaken for a response due date. A manager’s decision can sit in one thread while the operational task lives in another. Sensitive detail can be copied into a coordination field that only needs a short action description.

## What Heutrix would change

Heutrix would map the provider’s approved process, then configure only the action-management layer:

1. Link each action to an authorised source decision.
2. Give the action one owner, due date and next step.
3. Separate new, underway, waiting, blocked, ready for review and complete.
4. Restrict fields and views by role.
5. Prevent closure until the agreed evidence and approval are present.
6. Show leaders exceptions without exposing unnecessary complaint detail.

The provider would retain responsibility for the complaint process, response, outcome and obligations.

## The promised operating result

The coordinator would gain one action trail from decision to closure. Staff would see what they need to do next. Managers would see overdue and blocked actions instead of asking for a manual case-by-case update.

The workflow reduces uncertainty around handover; it does not claim to improve the complaint outcome itself.

## How the result would be proved

Synthetic cases would test several actions from one decision, reassignment, restricted access, an overdue item and attempted premature closure. Written acceptance would verify the agreed workflow behaviour and permissions.

## How Heutrix would scope it

Under **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**, this work would be fixed to one non-clinical follow-up workflow, with clear exclusions for investigation, professional advice and accountable decisions.

Bring the hardest administrative handover to a 20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call. Do not include complainant names, allegations or records.
