# Heutrix founder-delivery case-study collection

**Status:** Evidence-grounded editorial drafts  
**Updated:** 4 September 2026  
**Publication status:** Permission, attribution and final owner approval required

The current collection documents 15 case studies delivered by Heutrix across four Australian disability support providers. This archived version is superseded by the current case-study collection and source-evidence map.

They are **not 15 separate clients or engagements**. They show distinct operational problems solved across four providers in Heutrix delivery: process mapping, contractor onboarding, invoicing, reconciliation reporting, incident management and shift-note administration.

The organisation is anonymised in the editorial drafts. None of the work is presented as a Heutrix client engagement.

## Evidence reviewed

The editorial work drew on:

- the view-only support-worker onboarding and invoicing lifecycle board;
- two master Excel invoice and timesheet workbooks;
- four Power BI reconciliation report versions, including a cloud-connected version;
- 26 fortnightly timesheet snapshots and 26 payroll snapshots spanning approximately one operating year;
- a structured incident form, incident-management register and digital incident database field model; and
- a structured shift-note form-response model.

The source environments were inspected without copying real worker, client, participant, invoice, incident or shift-note records into this repository. See [Source evidence map](SOURCE-EVIDENCE-MAP.md) for the internal claim-by-claim boundary.

## Editorial standard

Each story:

- names one recognisable operational problem;
- describes the specific intervention evidenced by the source material;
- states the qualitative operating effect without inventing a metric;
- distinguishes workflow support from human judgement;
- avoids compliance, clinical, legal, payroll, tax or financial-accuracy claims; and
- explains how Heutrix would scope and test comparable work today.

The collection uses **founder-delivery experience**, not “Heutrix client results”. Quantified time, cost, error, revenue, submission, adoption or outcome claims remain withheld because no publishable baseline and post-change measurement set was reviewed.

## Publication gate

Before publishing any case study, the owner must:

1. confirm the attribution and which founders may be named;
2. confirm whether the source organisation may be named or must remain anonymised;
3. document the confidentiality and permission basis;
4. approve the exact qualitative claims;
5. remove or update any historical process wording that is no longer current;
6. retain the “prior founder role; not a Heutrix client engagement” label; and
7. obtain additional evidence before adding any quantified result.

Case 15 has an additional gate: locate and review the summarisation workflow definition, approved data path, prompt or configuration, human-review control and representative output evidence before describing the automation as independently verified.
