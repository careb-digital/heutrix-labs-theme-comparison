# Report review had one visible handover

> **Anonymised founder-delivery experience.** This case draws on reporting and allied-health operations experience within the Heutrix founding team. Identifying details and unverified performance figures are withheld. It is not presented as a Heutrix client engagement.

## The operational stakes

Reports required agreed inputs, administrative preparation, professional work and authorised review. The practice already had templates and source systems, but the readiness workflow sat around them in email, personal tasks and verbal updates.

The manager could see a due date without seeing the real constraint: missing information, unavailable professional time, returned work or an unresolved decision.

| Starting point | Delivered operating change | Observable result |
|---|---|---|
| One broad “in progress” state across preparation and review | Separate readiness, professional ownership, review and return states | Staff could see what was ready, what was blocked and who held the next action |

## The real constraint

The workflow treated the report as one task. In reality, each handover had a different owner and completion rule. Review comments created a new thread rather than returning a visible action.

## What changed

The founders structured the operating path around the decisions and handovers:

1. Start each cycle with the agreed inputs and due dates.
2. Define administrative readiness separately from professional completion.
3. Name the professional owner and authorised reviewer.
4. Return work with a specific next action and owner.
5. Show managers blockers and approaching deadlines rather than only open counts.
6. Keep clinical content in the approved source record, not the coordination view.

## The operating result

Administration could see what needed follow-up. Practitioners could see what was ready for their work. Reviewers had one controlled return path. The manager could investigate exceptions without opening every report or asking each person for an update.

The first improvement was a shared readiness definition; technology supported it after the workflow was clear.

## Proof boundary

This case supports the delivered handover and visibility pattern. It does not claim faster report completion, clinical accuracy, increased revenue or compliance. Any measured outcome would require the original records, dates and permission.

## How Heutrix would scope it today

Under **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**, the work would be bounded to preparation and review coordination. Acceptance would test a normal report, missing input, changed due date, returned work and role-based access.

Bring one recurring report and the handover that is hardest to see to a 20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call. Keep the description high-level and non-sensitive.
