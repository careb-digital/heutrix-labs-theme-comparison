# Corrective actions kept an owner, a due date and a closure trail

> **Anonymised founder-delivery experience.** This story is drawn from operational work completed by Heutrix founders in a prior care-provider role, before Heutrix began formal operations. It is not a Heutrix client engagement. The organisation is withheld pending publication approval.

The most important work often begins after an incident form is submitted. Actions must be assigned, completed, checked and connected back to the findings that created them.

| Starting point | Founder-built intervention | Operating effect |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-up could disappear into meeting notes or broad recommendations | A corrective-action section with responsibility, completion dates, remedial action and consultation records | Each agreed response had a place in the incident record and a visible route to closure |

## The real constraint

“Review the process” is not an operational action. It does not identify who owns the work, what completion means, when it is due or who confirms that the response addressed the finding.

The workflow needed to preserve the connection between investigation, contributing factors, selected controls and the work that followed.

## What the founders built

The incident materials linked several stages:

1. record immediate controls and the initial risk response;
2. document investigation findings and contributing factors;
3. identify preventive or corrective actions;
4. assign responsibility and a completion date;
5. record remedial action and relevant consultation;
6. capture worker feedback and continuous-improvement considerations; and
7. retain incident-manager sign-off in the control record.

The form also presented a hierarchy-of-controls prompt, encouraging the reviewer to document the selected response rather than leaving the action as an unstructured note.

## The operating result

The incident record extended beyond description into accountable follow-up. Managers had a defined place to record what was decided, who would act and when the action was completed. The structure made an incomplete response easier to see.

## Evidence and limits

The action, investigation and consultation sections were reviewed in the historical incident form and register. No completed records, outcome measures or audit findings were used. The material does not prove that actions were effective, timely or compliant, and the historical wording requires fresh owner and adviser review before reuse.

## How Heutrix would deliver this today

Heutrix would configure the organisation’s approved action model, not define its legal obligations. Synthetic cases would test multiple actions from one incident, reassignment, overdue work, blocked dependencies, reopening and attempted closure without the required review evidence.
