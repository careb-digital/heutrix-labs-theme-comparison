# Intake administration was ready before the first appointment

> **Anonymised founder-delivery experience.** This case draws on intake, customer-data and allied-health administration work undertaken by members of the Heutrix founding team. Identifying details are withheld. It is not presented as a Heutrix client engagement.

## The operational stakes

Several administrative items were needed before a first appointment. Some arrived through a form, some by email and some only after staff followed up. Reception could usually work out what was missing, but the answer was not visible to the practitioner or practice manager without checking several places.

As the appointment approached, planned follow-up became urgent chasing.

| Starting point | Delivered operating change | Observable result |
|---|---|---|
| Intake readiness inferred from forms, messages and memory | One administrative readiness state with item owners and an exception path | Staff could see what was missing and who held the next action before the appointment |

## The real constraint

The practice lacked an agreed definition of administrative readiness. Receipt did not always update the shared status. The appointment date was treated as the follow-up date. Exceptions were discussed but not carried into a visible workflow decision.

## What changed

The improvement separated coordination from clinical content:

- define the administrative items and categories supplied by the practice;
- assign an owner and follow-up date to each missing item;
- distinguish received, needs review, accepted and excepted;
- make the administrative readiness state visible to the relevant roles;
- route exceptions to the authorised client decision-maker; and
- keep patient and clinical content in the approved source record.

The workflow did not decide consent validity, clinical readiness or legal requirements. Those decisions remained with the practice and its qualified advisers.

## The operating result

Reception gained a clear follow-up list. Practitioners could see whether the administrative rule had been met without searching email. Managers saw approaching and excepted cases without asking staff to reconcile several trackers.

The team’s effort moved toward the genuinely incomplete cases rather than checking every case in the same way.

## Proof boundary

This case supports the qualitative readiness and ownership outcome. It does not claim fewer cancellations, a fixed time saving, patient outcomes or valid consent. Any such result would need a baseline and specific evidence.

## How Heutrix would scope it today

This pattern fits **Heutrix Workflow Transformation** for one intake-administration workflow. Synthetic acceptance cases would test a complete intake, a missing item, an authorised exception, a changed appointment and restricted access.

Bring the administrative handover—not patient forms or clinical details—to a 20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call.
