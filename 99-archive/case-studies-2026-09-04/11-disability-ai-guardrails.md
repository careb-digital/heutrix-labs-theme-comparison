# Staff got a usable yes, no or not-yet rule for AI

> **Illustrative Heutrix application.** This case demonstrates how Heutrix AI Guardrails would translate approved organisational decisions into everyday staff guidance. It is not presented as a client result or as legal, privacy, clinical, compliance or regulatory advice.

## The operational stakes

Staff are already experimenting with AI for administrative work. One person uses it to improve the wording of a generic checklist. Another wants to summarise internal notes. Managers have issued broad warnings, but staff still cannot answer the practical question: “Can I use AI for this task, with this information, in this tool?”

The desired outcome is not an impressive AI policy. It is a decision staff can apply before information is entered or an output is trusted.

| Starting point | Proposed operating change | Acceptance signal |
|---|---|---|
| General warnings and inconsistent judgement | Approved, conditional and prohibited use cases with a stop-and-escalate path | Staff can classify representative uses and identify the accountable reviewer |

## The real constraint

The organisation has not connected five decisions:

- the purpose of the task;
- the information involved;
- whether the tool is approved;
- the human review required; and
- who owns escalation and final judgement.

Without that connection, “use AI responsibly” leaves the hardest decision with the least-supported person.

## What Heutrix would change

**Heutrix AI Guardrails** would turn the organisation’s approved positions into a practical operating set:

1. A use-case register with approved, conditional and prohibited categories.
2. Information boundaries and input rules.
3. A named human reviewer for each approved use class.
4. Output checks tied to authoritative sources.
5. A stop-and-escalate path for uncertain uses.
6. Staff guidance written around real work moments, not abstract AI language.

AI would not make final clinical, safety, staffing, legal or compliance decisions. Public AI tools would not receive identifiable or sensitive information.

## The promised operating result

Staff would have a short decision path they can use before starting. Managers would be able to approve a narrow use without implying that all uses of the tool are acceptable. Accountable people would remain responsible for the final work.

The first useful milestone would be agreement on the highest-frequency use cases and the red lines—not a large policy document.

## How the result would be proved

Synthetic scenarios would test a clearly approved administrative draft, a conditional use, a prohibited input, an inaccurate output and an uncertain use. Acceptance would require staff to reach the organisation’s approved position and name the correct reviewer or escalation owner.

Bring two or three proposed AI uses at a general, non-sensitive level to a 20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call.
