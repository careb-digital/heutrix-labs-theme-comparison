# One intake view replaced the daily status hunt

> **Anonymised founder-delivery experience.** Members of the Heutrix founding team have delivered this type of workflow improvement in prior care-provider operating roles. Identifying details are withheld. This is not presented as a Heutrix client engagement.

## The operational stakes

Enquiries were arriving. Staff were responding. Yet a manager still had to ask three people to learn whether the next step belonged to administration, operations or the person who made the original contact.

The desired result was not “a new system”. It was much more practical: every enquiry should have one visible owner, one honest status and one next action.

| Starting point | Delivered operating change | Observable result |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries split across inboxes, calls and local lists | One structured intake view with agreed status and ownership rules | Staff could see what was waiting, blocked or ready for handover without reconstructing the story |

## The real constraint

The constraint was not the number of tools. It was that the team had no shared definition of the workflow.

“Waiting” could mean waiting for information, waiting for an internal decision or waiting for the next staff member to notice the message. Adding automation at that point would have accelerated ambiguity.

## What changed

The founders’ workflow-first approach began with the handovers:

1. Define the point at which an enquiry enters the shared workflow.
2. Separate administrative completeness from any accountable suitability decision.
3. Give every open record an owner, next action and next-action date.
4. Use short, agreed reasons for waiting or blocking.
5. Close the loop with a recorded handover or closure reason.

Only then was a simple tracking layer introduced around the approved systems already in use. The coordination record held the minimum information required to move the work; sensitive detail remained in its authorised source.

## The operating result

The team gained a shared queue instead of a collection of private interpretations. Administration could see what needed follow-up. Operations could see what was ready for handover. Managers could focus on exceptions rather than asking for a fresh status report.

The first useful win was clarity, not automation: agreed statuses made the work easier to discuss before any reminder or management view was added.

## Proof boundary

This case supports a qualitative claim about workflow visibility and ownership. It does not claim a conversion uplift, a fixed time saving or a participant outcome. Those claims require the original baseline, measurement period and permission to publish.

## How Heutrix would scope it today

This pattern maps to **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**: one bounded, non-clinical enquiry-to-intake workflow, typically delivered over 2–4 weeks once scope, access and decision-makers are ready. Acceptance would test owner, status, next action, handover and permission rules using synthetic or de-identified records first.

Bring a general description of where enquiries arrive and where ownership becomes unclear to a 20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call. No participant or other sensitive information is needed.
