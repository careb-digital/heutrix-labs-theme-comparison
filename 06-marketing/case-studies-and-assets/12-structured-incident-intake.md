# Incident reporting captured the facts once, in a consistent structure

> **Anonymised Heutrix case study.** Heutrix delivered this solution for an Australian disability support provider. The organisation is not named, and no identifying source material is included.

An incident narrative can be detailed and still leave the next person without the information needed to act. Heutrix turned incident capture into a structured workflow rather than a free-form account passed between inboxes.

| Starting point | Heutrix delivery | Operating effect |
|---|---|---|
| Important facts and follow-up details could be scattered through a narrative | A structured incident form covering facts, immediate response, assessment, reporting, investigation and actions | The record collected the information needed by later workflow stages in one consistent sequence |

## The real constraint

The form had to serve several moments at once: immediate fact capture, risk response, internal review, any authorised external-reporting decision, investigation and corrective action. If those stages were collapsed into one text box, critical follow-up could become difficult to see.

## What Heutrix delivered

The reviewed form separated the workflow into clear sections for:

- the people involved, affected and witnessing the event;
- date, time, place and incident description;
- immediate actions and risk controls;
- family, carer, police or medical contact where relevant;
- the organisation’s reportability decision and recorded reporting dates;
- investigation, findings and contributing factors;
- preventive and corrective actions; and
- consultation, feedback and continuous-improvement follow-up.

A later digital incident database used structured fields for incident type, severity, description, actions taken, witnesses, consent confirmation and submission metadata.

## The operating result

The form created a common starting point for the incident manager and the people responsible for follow-up. It connected the initial account to later assessment and action instead of treating submission as the end of the process.

## Approximate operating impact

- **Operational coverage:** 8 structured sections spanning initial facts, immediate action, review, investigation, corrective action and follow-up.
- **Indicative efficiency:** Approximately 10–20 minutes less clarification and information-chasing per incident record.

The efficiency range is an estimate based on the follow-up fields and workflow stages captured in the delivered structure. Client feedback supports the practical operation of the structured intake.

## Evidence and limits

The form and digital database structure were reviewed without opening completed incident records, and client feedback supports the practical operation of the structured intake. The form is a historical version with an old scheduled-review date, so it is evidence of the Heutrix-delivered workflow—not evidence that the wording is current or suitable for present use. This case makes no compliance, reportability, investigation-quality or incident-prevention claim.
