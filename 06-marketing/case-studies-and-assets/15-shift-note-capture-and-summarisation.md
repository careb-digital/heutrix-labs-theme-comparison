# Structured shift notes created the input for human-reviewed summaries

> **Anonymised Heutrix case study.** Heutrix delivered this solution for an Australian disability support provider. The organisation is not named, and no identifying source material is included.

Summarisation is only as useful as the notes underneath it. When shift information arrives in inconsistent shapes, an automated summary can make the writing shorter without making the record more dependable.

| Starting point | Heutrix delivery | Operating effect |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative notes and routine observations were difficult to review consistently across shifts | Structured digital note capture paired with a Heutrix-delivered summarisation automation and human review | Repeated observations could be organised into a concise review draft while responsibility stayed with a person |

## The real constraint

The challenge was not simply “use AI to write a summary”. The workflow had to separate factual entries, routine observations, free-text notes and the accountable interpretation of that information. It also had to recognise that shift notes may contain sensitive information that cannot be moved into an unapproved tool.

## What Heutrix delivered

The verified source-capture layer used a digital form and response sheet with fields for shift date and time, behavioural or shift details, routine observations, fluid intake and additional notes. The owner confirms that Heutrix also delivered an automation to summarise notes.

The responsible operating pattern is clear:

1. collect information in a consistent structure;
2. preserve the source notes;
3. generate a summary draft for a defined purpose;
4. require an authorised person to review the draft against the source; and
5. correct, approve or reject the summary before it is relied on.

## The operating result

Structured capture created a more workable input for review and summarisation. The automation could assist with synthesis, but it did not replace the source record or the person accountable for accuracy and escalation.

## Approximate operating impact

- **Operational coverage:** 6 core capture areas spanning shift timing, narrative details, routine observations, fluid intake and additional notes.
- **Indicative efficiency:** Approximately 20–40% less first-pass summary preparation effort while retaining human review.

The efficiency range is an estimate based on the structuring and draft-synthesis steps supported by the delivered workflow. Client feedback supports that the automation operated and assisted the review process.

## Evidence and limits

The digital form-response structure was reviewed without opening or copying completed notes. Client feedback supports that the Heutrix-delivered summarisation automation operated and assisted the review process. Its flow definition, prompt, model, output records, review log and approved data path were not independently reviewed, so the indicative range remains an estimate rather than a verified AI outcome. No sensitive note content, privacy-related detail or clinical, documentation-quality or safety claim may be published.
