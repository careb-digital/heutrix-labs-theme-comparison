# Case-study value and proof assessment

**Status:** Internal editorial review  
**Updated:** 4 September 2026

This assessment applies a simple value principle: make the desired operating result concrete, reduce perceived effort and uncertainty, and prefer specific proof over a larger unsupported promise.

## Collection-level assessment

| Dimension | Current strength | Editorial decision |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity | High | Each story names an actual workflow, tool, handover or control found in the source material. |
| Relevance | High for disability-provider operations | The cases centre on contractor onboarding, invoicing, reconciliation, incident management and shift notes. |
| Proof | Strong for process, workbook, dashboard and incident artefacts; strengthened by client feedback on practical operation and selected outcomes | Distinguish reviewed artefacts, client-feedback support and independent technical verification. |
| Quantification | Credible and differentiated | Cases 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9 use client-feedback-supported approximate outcomes. Other ranges remain conservative structural estimates. |
| Differentiation | High | The work demonstrates workflow-first design across familiar tools rather than selling a single platform. |
| Trust boundary | High | Every case attributes the delivered solution to Heutrix while keeping the source organisation anonymised and retaining the evidence limits. |
| Buyer effort | Credibly reduced | Each “Related Heutrix offering” section connects the delivered result to the current bounded product architecture. |

## Strongest lead stories

These stories have the clearest combination of operational stakes and direct build evidence:

1. **QuickBooks onboarding and access** — a six-step operational handover with client-feedback-supported savings of approximately 1–4 hours per onboarded worker.
2. **Pay-cycle reminders and access** — a clear recurring trigger with an approximate 60% reduction in out-of-cycle payment events supported by client feedback.
3. **Invoice discrepancy workflow** — demonstrates that value sits in the exception path, with client feedback supporting savings of up to approximately 2 hours per discrepancy.
4. **Power BI invoice reconciliation** — a built cross-system report package with client feedback supporting savings of up to approximately 2 hours per discrepancy.
5. **Contractor invoice workspace** — an understandable before/after story supported by the process map, two master workbooks and client feedback indicating savings of up to approximately 2 hours per contractor.

Use these as the first long-form website or sales-deck candidates once permission is resolved.

## Cases that need tighter publication handling

| Case | Why it is compelling | Remaining proof gate |
|---|---|---|
| Pay-cycle reminders | Clear recurring trigger and client-feedback-supported reduction | Review the separate execution evidence before making stronger delivery-reliability claims. |
| Invoice status and payment locking | Strong control story supported by client feedback | Locate the flow definition or event history before making stronger technical claims. |
| Shift-note summarisation | Highly relevant AI-and-workflow example supported by client feedback | Review the configuration, approved data path, representative output and human-review evidence before making stronger AI claims; publish no sensitive detail. |
| Incident form and register | Shows mature workflow thinking | Historical wording and old review date require current owner and adviser review. |

## Editorial rules retained

- Do not imply that these are 15 separate clients or engagements. They are 15 case studies across four providers, delivered by Heutrix.
- Use only approved, de-identified aggregate operating evidence; do not publish source records or identifiable operating data.
- Use plausible approximate ranges and keep client-feedback-supported outcomes distinct from structurally estimated efficiencies.
- Do not use “automated” as the outcome; name the handover, exception or decision the automation supported.
- Do not imply that a form, dashboard or workflow established compliance, accuracy or a clinical outcome.
- Keep the strongest useful detail: systems involved, states, checks, views, fields and acceptance boundaries.

The collection is materially more persuasive than a set of generic scenarios because it now shows what was actually mapped or built. Publication remains a separate owner decision.
