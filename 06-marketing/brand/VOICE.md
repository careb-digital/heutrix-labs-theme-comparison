# Heutrix Voice

## Voice definition

Heutrix sounds practical, credible, calm, direct and technically informed. It helps an operator understand a complicated workflow problem without making the work sound magical, intimidating or more certain than the evidence allows.

The clearest expression of the offer is:

> Diagnose the right operational problem, transform one bounded workflow, or put practical AI guardrails in place.

Start with the work that needs to function better. Introduce technology only when it supports a defined operating result.

## Product architecture and naming

The public offer architecture contains three products:

1. **Heutrix Diagnostics** — a paid, standalone decision product for situations where the priority, workflow boundary or feasible solution is not yet clear.
2. **Heutrix Workflow Transformation** — the primary implementation product for redesigning and implementing one bounded non-clinical operational workflow.
3. **Heutrix AI Guardrails** — a practical workplace AI governance product covering appropriate use, information boundaries, accountable human review, escalation and staff guidance.

Use each full product name in headings, navigation, offer cards, first references and formal calls to action. After the full name has been established, **Diagnostics**, **Workflow Transformation** and **AI Guardrails** may be used where repetition would sound unnatural.

Do not use older or informal labels as product names. In particular:

- replace **Workflow Diagnostic** with **Heutrix Diagnostics**;
- do not present a dashboard, tracker, form, visibility build, tailored system or automation as a separate Heutrix product;
- describe those items as possible delivery mechanisms within **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**; and
- use **Heutrix AI Guardrails**, not **Safe AI**, as the product name.

The relationship between the public names **Heutrix** and **Heutrix Labs** remains an owner decision. Until it is resolved, preserve the name used by the current authoritative asset and do not invent a new naming rule. The three product names above remain unchanged.

## How to describe each product

### Heutrix Diagnostics

Lead with clarity and decision value. The client should understand what is happening now, what matters most, what is feasible and what not to build.

Preferred framing:

- see what is slowing the work down and what to fix first;
- define the problem and workflow boundary;
- map the current state and rank bottlenecks, risks and dependencies;
- compare feasible improvement options, including a no-build option; and
- leave with a decision-ready implementation brief.

Do not describe Diagnostics as free discovery, an automatic first step or work that only has value if Heutrix later implements the recommendation.

### Heutrix Workflow Transformation

Lead with one bounded workflow and a usable operating result. This is the primary implementation product.

Preferred framing:

- turn one difficult workflow into a clearer, usable operating system;
- map, design, build, prove and hand over;
- clarify ownership, status, next action, handovers and management visibility;
- configure or build the simplest fit-for-purpose combination of process, forms, trackers, registers, dashboards, lightweight internal tools and automation; and
- test the agreed workflow, guide users and provide a maintainable handover.

Do not imply a whole-platform replacement, guaranteed integration, fixed efficiency gain, staff adoption outcome or support for a named system before feasibility and scope are confirmed.

### Heutrix AI Guardrails

Lead with usable workplace boundaries and accountable human judgement, not fear or generic AI enthusiasm.

Preferred framing:

- give staff clear, practical boundaries for using AI at work;
- define approved, conditional and prohibited uses;
- set information boundaries and input rules;
- assign human review, approval and escalation responsibilities; and
- provide practical staff guidance for day-to-day use.

Do not describe AI Guardrails as legal, privacy, clinical, compliance or regulatory advice. Do not imply that guardrails transfer accountability away from the organisation or its qualified professionals.

## Core traits

### Workflow-led

Begin with the operational problem, workflow owner, status, handover or decision—not the tool.

### Commercially clear

State who the product is for, what decision it supports, what is included, what is excluded and what happens next. Paid work is fixed in scope and price in writing before it begins.

### Privacy-aware

Treat information handling, access, permissions, tool approval, testing and human review as part of the service design. Ask for high-level, non-sensitive information at the enquiry stage.

### Technically credible

Explain systems and AI accurately, then translate them into operating consequences. Naming a platform does not promise support, integration or feasibility.

### Measured

Avoid hype, fear and unsupported certainty. A sound recommendation can be “do not automate this”, “no build is needed” or “Heutrix is not the right fit”.

## Segment emphasis

### Disability support providers — primary launch segment

Lead with recurring non-clinical operational workflows such as participant intake, worker onboarding, service commencement, reporting preparation, evidence and document tracking, internal admin coordination, document expiry, incident or complaint tracking and routine handovers.

Keep the language operational. Do not imply NDIS registration readiness, audit preparation, compliance advice, clinical advice or regulatory outcomes.

### Allied health practices — secondary launch segment

Use language about referral and intake tracking, enquiry follow-up, document collection, reporting preparation, staff handovers, practice visibility and responsible administrative AI use.

Do not give allied health equal strategic weight on general launch messaging unless the asset is specifically for that audience. Do not include GP clinics as a launch segment.

## Claims and evidence

- Describe the delivery process and agreed outputs, not unverified business outcomes.
- Do not promise integrations, time savings, revenue, adoption, uninterrupted services, compliance or regulatory results.
- Label synthetic and illustrative examples clearly and never present them as client outcomes.
- Do not invent clients, testimonials, credentials, founder claims, team capacity, entity details, insurance, service areas or supported systems.
- Evidence-refreshed case studies may be used only after their claim, confidentiality and owner-approval gates are closed. Keep the four source providers unnamed, identify the work as Heutrix delivery and disclose that the 15 case studies span four providers rather than 15 clients.
- Preserve the exact unit and approximate qualification of every approved outcome. Client feedback is supporting evidence, not a verbatim testimonial, independent audit or promise for future engagements.
- Do not publish a pricing page, public product prices or ranges, pricing navigation item or pricing CTA. Confirm scope, commercial terms and the applicable fee privately in writing after fit is established.
- Treat legal, privacy and operating documents as drafts until their approval is evidenced.

## Example voice

> We start by understanding how one operational workflow works today, where ownership or follow-up is breaking down and what a useful result would look like. The next step may be Heutrix Diagnostics, Heutrix Workflow Transformation, Heutrix AI Guardrails—or an honest recommendation not to proceed.

## CTA style

Use low-pressure, specific actions that match the current lead path:

- Request a free consultation
- Explore Heutrix Diagnostics
- Transform a workflow
- Put AI guardrails in place
- Request my 20-minute call
- View services

Describe the initial conversation as a **20-minute, no-obligation workflow fit call**. Until a working scheduler offers and confirms a real time, use **Request a free consultation** or **Request my 20-minute call** rather than **Book a call**. Once direct scheduling is live and tested, **Book a 20-minute workflow fit call** is appropriate.

Do not ask an unsure prospect to select a product before the call. Invite them to describe one operational problem at a high level without including patient, participant, worker or other sensitive information.

## LinkedIn voice and structure

For LinkedIn posts:

- open with a recognisable operating tension or question;
- teach one idea per post;
- favour concrete fields, decisions, handovers, examples and checklists over broad commentary;
- connect promotional posts to one of the three current products using its exact name;
- keep dashboards, trackers, forms, lightweight systems and automation inside the Workflow Transformation story rather than presenting them as separate offers;
- keep paragraphs short and readable on mobile;
- use two to four relevant hashtags rather than a large hashtag block;
- vary discussion, save, follow and fit-call calls to action;
- use emojis sparingly and only where they improve scanning;
- label synthetic examples in the first lines and on every related visual;
- label an approved case-study post as anonymised Heutrix delivery and keep its evidence limitation visible;
- put sources close to statistics or regulatory guidance;
- never imitate a founder's first-person story without that founder's review; and
- rewrite legacy calendar posts that refer to Workflow Diagnostic, public prices or older offer names before approval or publication.

The default LinkedIn tone is useful before promotional. A reader should learn something even if they never become a client.
