For allied health practice owners and managers

## Make one recurring admin workflow easier to see, manage and hand over.

Heutrix also works with Australian allied health practice owners and practice managers facing recurring referral, intake, reporting or document follow-up across people and systems. These are comparable to the administrative workflow problems we focus on for disability providers.

We define and test one non-clinical workflow around the practice’s existing systems. Agreed stages, owners and review points let staff find outstanding referrals or reports without rebuilding the status from inboxes, with instructions for handling exceptions and handing work over.

**Primary CTA:** Request a free consultation

**CTA support:** Bring a general, non-sensitive description of one recurring workflow. In a 20-minute, no-obligation call, we will assess fit and agree the smallest useful next step.

## Who this is for

A suitable starting point is recurring administrative friction, an accountable owner — such as a practice manager or intake lead — and the staff who use the workflow available to help map and test it.

Allied health remains a secondary audience. A suitable practice usually has:

- a recurring intake, referral, reporting, evidence or document workflow;
- multiple people, roles or systems touching the work;
- unclear ownership, inconsistent handover or poor management visibility;
- a workflow owner and decision-maker available;
- relevant staff able to participate in discovery, testing and training; and
- a problem important enough to fund as a fixed-scope improvement.

## Common allied health workflow problems

- Enquiries and intake follow-up handled differently by different staff
- Referral status and next actions spread across inboxes, spreadsheets and practice notes
- Reports, letters or administrative requests hard to track from request to review
- Documents chased through inbox searches and informal reminders
- Clinician and admin handovers varying by person or location
- Staff onboarding steps lacking clear ownership and completion visibility
- Managers unable to see work in progress, overdue items or bottlenecks
- AI tools being used without approved information boundaries or review rules

## Illustrative examples

**These are hypothetical workflow states, not client results or promised outcomes.**

The current Heutrix case-study collection relates to disability-provider delivery. These examples do not imply allied-health client proof.

### Referral and document follow-up

**Current-state pattern:** Referral details, contact attempts and missing documents sit in separate inboxes and trackers, leading staff to repeat the same status checks.

**Possible future-state pattern:** One scoped view could show referral stage, outstanding documents, owner and next action, so the intake lead can pick up the follow-up without searching each source again.

### Reporting preparation

**Current-state pattern:** Requests, inputs, drafts and reviews are spread across different places, making it difficult to tell what is ready and what is waiting.

**Possible future-state pattern:** Agreed stages and review owners could distinguish missing inputs from work ready for review, so the practice manager can direct follow-up to the person holding the next step.

### Staff onboarding

**Current-state pattern:** Onboarding tasks depend on repeated reminders, and responsibility becomes unclear when a step cannot be completed.

**Possible future-state pattern:** A tested checklist could name the completion check, owner and exception route for each step, with handover notes so the receiving team knows what is complete and what still needs attention.
