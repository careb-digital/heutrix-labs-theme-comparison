Add one resource link to the final resource paragraph. Never request a completed workbook or treat a download as contact permission.

### Post copy

A practical AI use-case register does not need to begin as a complex governance platform.

For each proposed or approved use, record:

- use-case name and purpose
- accountable business owner
- people who may be affected
- approved tool and account type
- information involved
- where inputs and outputs are stored
- how the output will be used
- human reviewer and review criteria
- approved, prohibited or review-required status
- decision date and next review date
- change or incident notes

The register makes two things visible:

1. Which uses have actually been assessed.
2. Whether a change in tool, data, purpose or consequence requires a new review.

Do not place sensitive prompts or personal information in the register itself. It is a control record, not a copy of the work.

Keep it short enough to maintain and important enough to review.

The free AI Guardrails Check screens one administrative use case for controls needing attention. Its companion AI Guardrails Staff Starter Pack provides a PDF guide, workbook, register and staff checklists. No email address is required.

A screening result does not approve a tool, use case or data type.

## P41 — Tool approval is not use-case approval

- Publish: Week 14 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

“We use an approved AI tool” is only the start of the decision.

Approval should cover the combination of:

- **Tool** — which product, plan, settings and account?
- **Use** — what exact task is it supporting?
- **Information** — what can and cannot enter it?
- **Output** — where will the result go and what could it affect?
- **Reviewer** — who checks it, using what criteria?
- **Change** — what triggers reassessment?

A platform approved for generic drafting may not be approved for personal information.

A feature approved for an internal first draft may not be approved for direct external communication.

A tool assessed last year may have changed its terms, storage, model or controls.

Govern the use case, not the logo on the login screen.

## P42 — A practical human-review checklist for AI-assisted admin

### Post copy

Before using an AI-assisted administrative output, the reviewer should be able to answer:

- Is the output factually accurate?
- Is anything important missing?
- Are names, dates, amounts and references correct?
- Does it rely on a source that can be checked?
- Has personal or sensitive information been handled through an approved process?
- Is the tone appropriate for the audience?
- Does the output overstate certainty?
- Is a qualified person still making the important decision?
- Is the approved final version stored in the correct place?
- Should an error, near miss or unexpected output be recorded or escalated?

Not every criterion applies to every low-risk use case. The organisation should define the relevant test before use.

The reviewer must have enough time, context and authority to reject the output.

Human review is not clicking “accept”. It is an accountable quality step.
