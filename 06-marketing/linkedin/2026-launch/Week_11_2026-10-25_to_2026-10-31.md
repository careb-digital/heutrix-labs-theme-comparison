# Week 11 — Handover, closure and workflow debt

## P31 — Most handover failures are design failures

- Publish: Week 11 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

“Communication needs to improve” is often true and still too vague to fix the handover.

Inspect the design:

- Is the sending role clear?
- Is the receiving role clear?
- What minimum information is required?
- Where is it recorded?
- How does the receiver know an action is waiting?
- By when should they respond?
- What happens if information is missing?
- How is acceptance or completion confirmed?

If those answers live in habit rather than the workflow, a reminder to “communicate better” puts the burden back on individual memory.

A strong handover creates a shared moment of responsibility: one person has provided what is needed, another has accepted the next action, and both can see the status.

Choose one repeated handover this week. Do not ask who dropped it.

Ask what the workflow made easy to miss.

## P32 — Give every workflow a definition of done

- Publish: Week 11 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: checklist document
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`

### Post copy

An operational item can leave the active list for several reasons.

It was completed.

It was declined.

It was withdrawn.

It was transferred to another workflow and the receiving owner accepted it.

It is paused with a named owner and review date.

Or someone simply stopped looking at it.

A useful definition of done separates completed or deliberately closed work from paused work and abandoned work.

Paused work is still open. Keep its owner, next action and review date visible. A transfer closes the original item only when the receiving owner accepts responsibility.

For a referral, intake, onboarding or internal request, define:

- the permitted closure outcomes
- who can close the item
- what minimum record is required
- what must be communicated
- where any ongoing action moves
- when closed items are reviewed or retained

Closure matters because open work affects workload, follow-up, reporting and trust.

If “done” is not defined, the list will contain completed work, abandoned work and real work in the same status.

Save this checklist for your next workflow review.

#DefinitionOfDone #WorkflowManagement #ProcessImprovement

### Document brief

Create six pages: cover; allowed closure outcomes with paused work kept open; authorised closer; minimum record; communication and onward action; close with “Done is a decision, not a disappearing row.”

## P33 — Workflow debt accumulates quietly

- Publish: Week 11 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

Workflow debt is the extra admin a team keeps paying because a temporary workaround became normal.

It looks like:

- copying the same information between systems
- maintaining a side spreadsheet “for now”
- asking one person to reconcile every exception
- manually rebuilding a report each month
- using extra meetings to create visibility
- relying on reminders because ownership is unclear
- teaching new staff the workaround instead of fixing the process

Each action may be small.

Together they consume attention and make change harder.

Create a workflow-debt list with three fields:

1. The repeated workaround.
2. Why it still exists.
3. The next decision needed.

Do not try to clear the entire list at once.

Choose the debt attached to a frequent, important workflow and understand it before buying a solution.
