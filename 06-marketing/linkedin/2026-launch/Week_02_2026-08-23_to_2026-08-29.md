# Week 2 — Disability-provider workflow field notes

## P04 — Participant intake rarely breaks in one dramatic moment

- Publish: Week 2 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability support providers
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

Participant intake rarely breaks in one dramatic moment.

It becomes harder to manage through a series of small, invisible handovers.

An enquiry arrives. Information is requested. A fit decision waits. Someone follows up. A start date is discussed. Another team needs to act.

At each step, the same five questions matter:

- What is the current status?
- Who owns the next action?
- What is the team waiting for?
- When should it be reviewed again?
- What outcome will close the item?

If the answers sit across inboxes, spreadsheets, chat messages and staff memory, the team may be busy while the workflow remains difficult to see.

This is not about putting every participant detail in a dashboard. A useful operating view can hold a controlled reference, owner, status, next action and review date while sensitive records remain in the approved source system.

Take one recent intake and mark each handover from enquiry to outcome.

Where did someone have to reconstruct the story? That is often the first design problem worth examining.

## P05 — “Starting soon” is not a service-commencement status

- Publish: Week 2 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability support providers
- Format: simple readiness diagram plus text
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: keep the example operational and do not imply compliance, safeguarding or service-readiness assurance.

### Post copy

“Starting soon” is not a service-commencement status.

Neither is “almost ready”.

Those phrases hide the decisions an accountable person still needs to make.

A practical commencement view should show, at an appropriate level:

- the intended start date;
- the current owner;
- what is complete;
- what is still waiting;
- the next action and review date;
- any exception requiring a decision; and
- who confirms readiness under the provider’s approved process.

The workflow should not infer readiness from a green cell or a completed checklist. A named person still makes the accountable decision.

Nor does every sensitive record need to be copied into the view. Often the operating layer needs only enough controlled information to show what happens next.

Before the next commencement, finish this sentence:

**Ready means ______, and ______ confirms it.**

If the team completes the sentence differently, the issue is bigger than a missing reminder.

#DisabilityProviders #ServiceCommencement #OperationalReadiness

### Asset brief

Create a mobile-first two-panel diagram:

1. “Starting soon” → hidden ambiguity.
2. Owner + status + next action + review date + named readiness decision.

Label the visual “Illustrative operating view—not compliance or service-readiness assurance.”

## P06 — Illustrative: the best automation decision was not to build

- Publish: Week 2 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider leaders and operations managers
- Format: text; optional three-card document
- Status: `owner_approval_required`
- Human check: approve the illustrative proof asset before use. Keep the label in the opening and on every visual.

### Post copy

**Illustrative Heutrix application—not a client result.**

The request sounds straightforward:

“Can we automate reminders for missing information?”

Then Heutrix Diagnostics finds three problems:

- different teams disagree about what “complete” means;
- nobody owns the exception decision; and
- the data needed to trigger the reminder is not recorded consistently.

Automating now would send conflicting reminders faster.

The more useful first move is no build:

1. Agree one decision rule.
2. Name the person who approves exceptions.
3. Run a short baseline using non-sensitive operational data.
4. Reassess whether automation is still justified.

That is what good diagnosis should do: compare process clarification, a manual control, a lightweight workflow and automation—then recommend the smallest option supported by the evidence.

The value is the decision, even when the answer is “not yet”.

Save this question for the next automation request:

**Is the rule stable enough to automate?**

#HeutrixDiagnostics #DisabilitySupport #WorkflowDesign
