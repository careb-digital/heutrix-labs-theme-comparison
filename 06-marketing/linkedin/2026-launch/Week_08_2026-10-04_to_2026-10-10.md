## P22 — Enquiry-to-service-start field guide

- Publish: Week 8 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability support providers

- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`

- Publisher link: use the verified public origin plus `/resources/enquiry-to-service-start-starter-kit`. Add one resource link to the final resource paragraph. Never request a completed workbook or treat a download as contact permission.

### Post copy

An enquiry-to-service-start workflow needs more than a contact form and a commencement checklist.

It needs a visible path between them.

A practical operating view may need to show:

- enquiry received
- service and location request
- initial fit review
- information still required
- current owner and next contact
- internal decision waiting
- proposed start timing
- readiness steps
- outcome communicated
- commenced, paused, declined or closed

The exact steps and decisions must come from the provider's approved processes and qualified requirements.

The workflow-design question is narrower:

Can the accountable people see where the request is, what is blocking it and who acts next—without exposing unnecessary participant information?

Map one recent enquiry from arrival to outcome. Mark every point where the status had to be reconstructed from calls, messages or memory.

That is the improvement list.

The free Enquiry-to-Service-Start Planner helps you review the handovers and create a starter map to discuss with your team. Its companion Visibility Starter Kit includes a PDF guide and workbook with status definitions and synthetic test records. No email address is required.

The result supports a workflow discussion; it does not confirm readiness to commence a service.

## P23 — Document expiry is a workflow, not a date column

- Publish: Week 8 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability support providers
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

A spreadsheet can show that a document expires next month.

The workflow decides what happens before and after that date.

For each controlled expiry process, clarify:

- how much notice is needed
- who receives the first action
- where the replacement is submitted
- who reviews it
- what happens if it is incomplete
- when the issue is escalated
- what operational restrictions apply, if any
- what closes the item

Those rules must reflect the provider's approved policies and qualified requirements. A generic tracker cannot decide them.

Once the rules are clear, the system can support reminders, status, evidence location and exceptions.

Without the workflow, a date column tells you a problem exists. It does not help the right person resolve it.

## P24 — Synthetic before-and-after: service commencement

- Publish: Week 8 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability support providers
- Format: before/after diagram
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: keep the word “synthetic” prominent. This is not a client result or compliance model.

### Post copy

**Synthetic example—not a client case study or compliance model.**

Imagine a disability provider coordinating new service commencements through email, chat messages and separate checklists.

**Before**

- The proposed start date is known, but readiness is not visible.
- Different teams hold different parts of the story.
- Missing actions are discovered in follow-up meetings.
- “Almost ready” has no agreed meaning.
- The accountable person has to chase updates.

**Possible future state**

- One controlled view shows the commencement stage.
- Every open action has an owner and review date.
- Readiness criteria come from approved provider processes.
- Exceptions are visible to the accountable decision-maker.
- Sensitive records remain in their approved systems.
- Commencement is confirmed by a named person, not inferred from a green status.

The useful system is not the one with the most fields.

It is the one that helps the accountable team see what is ready, what is not and what must happen next.
