# Tailored LinkedIn content strategy

## Outcome

Use LinkedIn to make Heutrix the practical, privacy-aware first conversation for Australian disability support providers when one recurring non-clinical workflow is slow, inconsistent or difficult to see. Build secondary recognition among allied health practices with the same workflow pattern.

The first 18 weeks should build recognition, trust and qualified conversations. Follower growth is useful only when it increases access to the right operators, decision-makers and referral partners. The more important result is evidence that those people recognise the problem, understand the three ways Heutrix can help and take a relevant next step.

## Strategic job

The campaign must move the right reader through four beliefs:

1. **Recognition:** “This is an operating problem we actually have.”
2. **Reframing:** “The answer may be a clearer workflow, not another platform.”
3. **Trust:** “Heutrix uses judgement, protects boundaries and will say no-build or no-fit.”
4. **Action:** “I can bring one high-level problem to a 20-minute workflow fit call without preparing a technical brief or sharing sensitive information.”

## Positioning

### Category

Workflow improvement, practical systems and safe operational AI.

### Point of view

> Workflow first. Technology second.

### Market promise

Heutrix reviews how work happens now, identifies where information, ownership or follow-up is being lost, and recommends the simplest practical improvement.

### Product architecture

| Reader situation | Product | Message to reinforce |
|---|---|---|
| “We know operations are messy, but not what to fix first.” | **Heutrix Diagnostics** | Define the problem, compare feasible options and leave with a decision-ready implementation brief; the answer may be no build. |
| “We know which workflow needs to work better.” | **Heutrix Workflow Transformation** | Redesign, implement, test and hand over one bounded non-clinical workflow. This is the primary implementation product; process-only improvement can qualify. |
| “Staff use AI, but our boundaries and review controls are unclear.” | **Heutrix AI Guardrails** | Define approved, conditional and prohibited uses, information boundaries, accountable review and practical staff guidance. |

Do not publish prices, starting ranges or pricing-led calls to action. Approval of the current pricing and margin model controls private quoting. Dashboards, forms, trackers, automation and lightweight internal tools are possible delivery mechanisms within Workflow Transformation, not separate Heutrix products.

The offers are independent entry points, not a mandatory sequence. A clear workflow can go directly to Workflow Transformation; a defined governance need can go directly to AI Guardrails. Agree a limited investigation boundary for Diagnostics. New Diagnostics and AI Guardrails operating definitions in the 8 September architecture remain subject to delivery validation.

Use **Diagnose → Triage → Build → Handover** as the public method. Mapping/baseline sit in Diagnose, design and acceptance criteria in Triage, implementation/testing in Build, and guidance/ownership in Handover. Diagnose here does not mean a separate Diagnostics purchase.

### Deliberate boundaries

Heutrix is not presented as a clinical-AI provider, legal adviser, NDIS registration consultant, official auditor or compliance assurance service. It does not promise savings, productivity, registration or regulatory outcomes.

### Current evidence position

The case-study collection documents 15 case studies delivered by Heutrix across four anonymised Australian disability support providers. It does not represent 15 clients or separate engagements.

Cases 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9 contain approximate outcomes supported by client feedback and reviewed artefacts. Other efficiency ranges remain structural estimates and should not be promoted into LinkedIn result claims. None of the outcomes is independently audited or a promise for future work.

Janith approved the current 15 anonymised narratives and qualified ranges on 5 September 2026. P16 and P34 use that existing approval and still need normal post/asset review; changed or stronger claims require renewed proof approval. Keep the source organisation unnamed, use no source screenshots or records, retain the stated unit and limitation, and do not turn client feedback into a testimonial. The current evidence is disability-provider delivery and must not be relabelled as allied-health proof.

## Priority audiences

| Audience | Typical role | What they are experiencing | What the content should help them recognise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary decision-maker | Disability-provider owner, chief executive, general manager, operations lead or manager | Intake, service commencement, reporting preparation, evidence/document tracking or handovers cross people and systems without clear status or ownership | One bounded workflow can be improved without turning the work into a registration, audit or whole-platform project |
| Primary internal influencer | Disability-provider administrator, coordinator, team lead, digital or quality lead | Repeated chasing, duplicate entry, unclear exceptions and reporting work | They can describe a high-friction workflow in terms that support a useful decision |
| Secondary decision-maker | Allied-health practice owner, practice manager or operations manager | Referrals, enquiries, reporting inputs or document collection are spread across systems and memory | The problem may be a workflow and ownership issue, not a reason to replace the practice-management system |
| Referral partner | Sector adviser, accountant, IT provider, software implementer or consultant | A client has an operational problem outside the referrer's exact scope | Heutrix can diagnose or improve one workflow without automatically replacing existing tools |

General launch posts should lead with disability-provider language or a transferable operating problem. Allied-health posts should be deliberate secondary segments, not an equal parallel campaign. GP clinics are out of scope for launch.

## Content pillars

| Pillar | Share | Job | Recurring series |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Disability workflow field notes | 30% | Create recognition in the primary segment | Intake; Service Commencement; Reporting Preparation; Evidence and Documents |
| Workflow diagnosis and design | 25% | Teach the method and show when not to build | Workflow First; Before You Automate; Definition of Done |
| Heutrix AI Guardrails | 20% | Build trust through practical boundaries and accountable review | Approved / Conditional / Prohibited; Human Review in Practice |
| Workflow Transformation mechanics | 15% | Show how visibility, handovers and the smallest useful system support action | Make Work Visible; Diagnose–Triage–Build–Handover |
| Trust, proof and offer clarity | 10% | Make judgement and product fit concrete with approved anonymised delivery evidence or clearly labelled synthetic material | Anonymised Heutrix Delivery; How We Work; Illustrative Walkthrough; No-Build Decision |

Allied-health examples sit within the most relevant pillars and should generally remain a minority of the total feed. At least three posts should teach or demonstrate useful judgement for every one direct commercial post.

## Four campaign phases

### Weeks 1–4: Establish recognition and trust

- Introduce Heutrix and its boundaries.
- Repeat the workflow-first point of view in different practical forms.
- Show recognisable disability-provider problems first.
- Explain the three Heutrix products without asking an unsure reader to choose one.
- Introduce safe-AI principles without fear or hype.

Primary signal: impressions and engagement from disability-provider roles, saves, profile views and comments that name a workflow pattern without disclosing sensitive information.

### Weeks 5–8: Teach the method

- Explain current state, future state, ownership, status and exceptions.
- Publish field guides for referrals, intake, follow-up, onboarding and service commencement.
- Use the approved Power BI reconciliation narrative in P16 after normal post review, retaining the approximate per-discrepancy unit and evidence limits.
- Ask one or two narrow audience questions.

Primary signal: saves, quality comments, direct messages and repeated engagement from the target sectors.

### Weeks 9–13: Demonstrate judgement

- Compare spreadsheets, forms, trackers, automations and tailored systems.
- Explain what not to automate.
- Use approved anonymised Heutrix delivery evidence where it matches the subject and clearly labelled synthetic examples where it does not.
- Make service boundaries, handover and the fit-call process transparent.

Primary signal: fit questions, referral-partner interest and messages that use Heutrix's language (“one workflow”, “visibility”, “handover”).

### Weeks 14–18: Convert and learn

- Address objections and explain who each Heutrix product fits.
- Explain that scope, price and terms are confirmed privately in writing after fit is established.
- Help organisations select the next workflow worth improving.
- Review content performance and choose the next 90-day focus.

Primary signal: qualified fit calls, diagnostic enquiries, conversion by sector and clear evidence of which content pillars attract the right audience.

## Publishing model

### Cadence

- Three Page posts per week: Monday, Wednesday and Friday.
- Assign the actual start date only after the outreach gates, publisher, response owner and lead path are ready.
- Initial test time: 8:15 am in `Australia/Sydney` time.
- Review timing and cadence after Week 4; keep the learning, not the original assumption.
- Publish the recap after 18 actual campaign weeks, not on the old December date.

Three posts a week is the proposed starting cadence. Test it against actual production and response capacity; the August research is background, not a current platform-performance guarantee. Founders should add reach through genuine commentary and selective re-sharing, not identical copy-paste reposts.

### Format mix across 52 posts

- Keep most posts text-led or pair them with one simple original image.
- Use documents or carousels when a checklist, sequence or contrast is genuinely easier to save and understand visually.
- Keep this campaign's planned visual assets to static images and document pages that can be generated in either ChatGPT Images or Gemini Nano Banana Pro; use native polls only where the calendar specifies them.
- Use polls to learn which workflow guidance the audience wants, not as generic engagement bait.
- Use founder, case-study and campaign-reflection posts only when the underlying evidence, confidentiality permission and measured learning exist.

If visual production capacity is limited, publish the copy as text rather than delaying the cadence. Original diagrams and relevant explanatory graphics are preferred over generic stock imagery.

### Post construction

1. Open with a recognisable operating tension, not a slogan.
2. Make the cost of the current confusion visible without manufacturing urgency.
3. Explain one idea only.
4. Use a short example, model, contrast or question.
5. End with one low-pressure next action.
6. Add two to four relevant hashtags only.

Avoid engagement bait. A question should help Heutrix learn how the audience describes the problem, not merely invite easy agreement.

### CTA ladder

| Stage | CTA examples |
|---|---|
| Awareness | “Which step causes the most chasing in your team?” |
| Usefulness | “Save this for your next workflow review.” |
| Consideration | “Talk to Heutrix” or “Message Heutrix with a high-level workflow pattern—without sensitive information.” |
| Conversation request with current email path | “Talk to Heutrix.” Explain that the visitor reviews and sends an email draft and a time is agreed separately. |
| Conversion after scheduler and lead path pass testing | “Book a 20-minute workflow fit call.” |

No more than one in four posts should use a direct commercial CTA.

## Founder amplification

The Company Page is the source. The founders extend the conversation.

For each Page post:

- one relevant founder adds a first-person observation or example in their own words;
- the other founders engage only when they have something useful to add;
- no engagement pod, repeated comment script or identical repost text;
- the named response owner answers comments using the approved service level;
- any real client or operator story requires permission and de-identification review before publication; the current case-study permission does not include a client name, verbatim testimonial, source screenshot or identifying context.

The 5 September approved summaries cover Janith (workflow implementation and experience helping build a disability support business), Rochelle (project delivery and disability-provider administration/operations), Sach (data and analytics) and Shash (backend engineering/infrastructure). The approved origin is first-hand experience running a disability and allied health services organisation. Use only those bounded claims; detailed biographies, portraits, tenure and operational ownership remain unapproved. Each founder reviews their own first-person contribution.

## Community and comment rules

- Never ask for patient, participant, worker, client or commercially sensitive details.
- If a commenter starts sharing sensitive details, ask them to stop and move to a controlled conversation without repeating the information.
- Do not answer a legal, clinical, employment, NDIS compliance or regulatory question as advice.
- Distinguish practical workflow observations from formal requirements.
- Correct material errors openly and record what changed.
- Do not turn every comment into a sales reply.

## Measurement plan

### Baseline

Record before Post 1:

- Page completion status;
- follower count;
- founder profiles linked to the Page;
- Page CTA destination;
- website sessions and fit-call baseline, if measurement is available;
- number of existing qualified contacts invited to follow.

### Per-post fields

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Impressions | Basic reach, interpreted alongside audience quality |
| Reactions, comments, reposts and saves | What kind of value the post created |
| Engagement rate | Comparison across posts of different reach |
| Target-role interactions | Whether the right people, not just friends, are responding |
| Page views and follower change | Whether content creates brand curiosity |
| Link clicks or CTA clicks | Consideration behaviour |
| Qualified DMs and fit calls | Commercial intent |
| Audience segment and role | Whether the primary disability-provider audience is responding and whether secondary allied-health content attracts the intended roles |
| Pillar and format | Which subject and delivery style work |

### Review gates

| Gate | Decision |
|---|---|
| Week 4 | Keep or change time, hook style and format mix; confirm whether disability-provider roles are seeing and engaging with the content |
| Week 8 | Increase the top two useful pillars; retire weak generic topics; assess Page actions and message quality |
| Week 13 | Decide whether any organically strong, proof-safe post is worth modest amplification after approval |
| Week 18 | Select the next editorial theme and set a qualified-conversation target from observed data |

Do not set public follower or lead promises before the baseline exists. Internal targets should be created after the first four weeks of real Page data.

## Activation gate before Post 1

Do not schedule the launch sprint until:

- public brand treatment, founder/trust material and Page details are approved;
- the Page CTA and website lead path reach a monitored destination and show honest success and error states;
- one person owns approval, publishing, comments, measurement and lead handoff, with backup cover;
- the 20-minute workflow fit-call path and follow-up rules are tested;
- no post publishes an unapproved price, tool capability, client result, founder claim or legal assurance;
- P16 and P34 preserve the existing 5 September proof approval and pass normal adaptation/asset review against the current evidence map; and
- any illustrative or synthetic proof asset has an owner approval recorded.

## Page setup before Post 1

- Complete logo, banner, company description, location, industry, website and CTA.
- Choose the public brand name: Heutrix or Heutrix Labs.
- Link all confirmed founder profiles to the Page.
- Add the Page link to founder signatures and relevant owned channels.
- Confirm who publishes, who approves, who responds and who covers leave.
- Create a simple measurement sheet and source register.
- Confirm the fit-call destination and privacy-safe enquiry wording. Use “Talk to Heutrix” with the current visitor-sent email mechanism. Do not promise automatic receipt or a booked time.
- Prepare alt text and document accessibility for visual posts.

## Editorial rule

Every post should pass this test:

> Would an operator learn something useful even if they never buy from Heutrix?

If not, improve the post or replace it.
