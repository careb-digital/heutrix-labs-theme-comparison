# Week 10 — Choose the smallest useful system

## P28 — When a spreadsheet is good enough

- Publish: Week 10 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

Not every spreadsheet is a problem.

A spreadsheet may be the right tool when:

- the workflow is small and stable
- one accountable owner maintains it
- access is controlled appropriately
- the fields have clear definitions
- users are not creating competing copies
- there are few complex rules or integrations
- someone reviews data quality
- there is a clear backup and handover process

The warning signs are different:

- multiple versions
- broken formulas nobody owns
- sensitive information copied unnecessarily
- status values used inconsistently
- too many manual reminders
- reporting that needs reconstruction
- the workflow stops when one person is away

Do not replace a spreadsheet because it looks simple.

Replace or redesign it when the operating risk, volume, permissions or maintenance needs have outgrown the design.

The question is not “Is this a spreadsheet?”

It is “Can this team rely on it?”

## P29 — When a form plus tracker is the smallest useful system

- Publish: Week 10 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: simple architecture diagram
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`

### Post copy

Sometimes a workflow does not need a custom app.

It needs a controlled entry point and a trustworthy work list.

A simple form-plus-tracker design can work when:

1. A request enters through one approved form.
2. Required fields reduce avoidable back-and-forth.
3. The submission creates one controlled record.
4. A named owner receives the next action.
5. Status, due date and exceptions are visible.
6. Restricted detail remains in the appropriate source system.
7. The item is deliberately closed.

This pattern can support internal requests, general enquiries, document collection or another defined administrative workflow.

It still needs decisions about access, privacy, retention, notifications, data quality, ownership and maintenance.

Simple technology is not the same as casual design.

The smallest useful system is the one the team can understand, control and maintain.

#WorkflowAutomation #InternalSystems #OperationalDesign

### Asset brief

Show the sequence: approved form → controlled record → owner/work queue → review/exception → close. Add a separate protected source-system icon for restricted information; do not show patient or participant fields.

## P30 — When a tailored internal system is justified

- Publish: Week 10 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

A tailored internal system may be justified when a defined workflow has outgrown forms, spreadsheets and simple automation.

Look for a combination of needs:

- several roles need different views or permissions
- the workflow has multiple stages and exceptions
- important rules must be applied consistently
- data needs to move between approved systems
- volume makes manual coordination unreliable
- the team needs a controlled audit trail or change history
- reporting must come from the same operating record
- the organisation can own maintenance after handover

Even then, custom does not mean “build everything”.

Start with the smallest end-to-end workflow that can be tested with real users and safe data.

Define acceptance criteria, access, failure handling, human decisions and handover before development begins.

If the workflow is not yet clear, diagnose it first. A custom build is an expensive way to discover what the process should have been.
