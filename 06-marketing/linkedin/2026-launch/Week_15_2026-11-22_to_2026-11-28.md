# Week 15 — Three common objections

## P43 — “We already have practice-management software”

- Publish: Week 15 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: allied health practices
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

“We already have practice-management software.”

Good. The first question should be how to use the existing environment better—not how to replace it.

Workflow problems can still exist around a capable core system:

- enquiries arrive through several channels
- referral ownership is unclear before a record is created
- staff use inconsistent statuses
- document requests are tracked in email
- handovers happen in chat messages
- managers rebuild an operating view outside the system
- one integration or permission gap creates repeated work

The right improvement may be configuration, training, a clearer process, a controlled tracker around the edge or a better reporting view. An integration is an option only after feasibility, permissions, vendor limits and information handling are assessed.

Replacement is only one option, and often the most disruptive.

Map the end-to-end workflow across people and systems before deciding that the main platform is the problem.

## P44 — “Our team just needs more training”

- Publish: Week 15 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

Training is useful when the expected process is clear.

It is less useful when staff are being trained into ambiguity.

Before scheduling another refresher, check:

- Is there one agreed workflow?
- Are roles and handovers explicit?
- Do status labels mean the same thing to everyone?
- Can staff find the current instruction?
- Does the system support the expected action?
- Are exceptions documented?
- Is there time and authority to complete the work?
- Does the feedback loop show where the design is failing?

If capable people repeatedly make the same mistake, the cause may be the interface, information, workload, rule or handover—not a lack of attention.

Fix the design issue and train the changed workflow.

Do not use training as a permanent workaround for a process the team cannot reliably follow.

## P45 — “We are too small for process work”

- Publish: Week 15 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

Small teams already have processes.

They are simply more likely to live in conversation, memory and one person's way of doing things.

That can work while the volume is low and the same people are present.

It becomes harder when:

- someone takes leave
- demand increases
- a new person joins
- an exception occurs
- reporting is needed
- sensitive information requires tighter access
- the owner becomes the answer to every question

Process work for a small organisation should be proportionate.

Choose one repeated, important workflow. Define the trigger, owner, next action, exception and finish. Keep the system as simple as the risk and volume allow.

You do not need an enterprise transformation program.

You need one reliable way to complete work that matters.
