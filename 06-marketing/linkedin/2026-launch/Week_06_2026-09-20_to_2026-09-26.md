## P16 — An invoice dashboard should lead to a decision—not another search

- Publish: Week 6 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`
- Human check: the underlying anonymised narrative and qualified range were approved by Janith on 5 September in the [proof register](../../case-studies-and-assets/PROOF-ASSET-REGISTER.md). Check this adaptation against the [source-evidence map](../../case-studies-and-assets/SOURCE-EVIDENCE-MAP.md); normal post review remains. Re-review any stronger or changed claim.

### Post copy

**Anonymised Heutrix case study.**

For one Australian disability support provider, invoice reconciliation required information from contractor workbooks and QuickBooks Time to be compared before discrepancies could be investigated.

Heutrix delivered a Power BI reporting workflow with dedicated views for invoice totals, time records, hours, kilometres, workers, clients, fortnights and variances.

The useful result was not “more charts”. It was a clearer exception path:

- identify the discrepancy;
- compare the relevant sources;
- isolate what needed investigation; and
- give the reviewer a defined next action.

Client feedback supports up to approximately two hours less reconciliation and investigation effort per discrepancy.

That outcome is approximate and not an independently audited time study. The report does not establish that either source was complete or accurate, and it does not prove an error-rate or financial benefit.

This is one of 15 case studies documenting Heutrix delivery across four unnamed disability support providers. It is not a forecast of savings for another organisation.

This is the kind of bounded reporting and exception workflow that sits within **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**.

Before building your next dashboard, write down the decision it should support and the exception path it should trigger.

## P17 — Four fields that make waiting work visible

- Publish: Week 6 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: single graphic plus text
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`

### Post copy

Many operational trackers become more useful when every open item has four fields:

**Status** — where is it now?

**Owner** — who is accountable for the next action?

**Next action** — what specifically needs to happen?

**Due or review date** — when should someone look again?

This is not enough for every workflow, but it is a strong test.

If an item is marked “pending” with no owner, action or date, it is not being tracked. It is being stored.

Keep sensitive detail in the approved source system. The operating view may only need a reference, controlled status and action data.

Try the four-field test on one spreadsheet this week.

How many rows can someone act on without asking for the story again?

#ProcessImprovement #OperationalVisibility #WorkManagement

### Asset brief

Create one mobile-first card with four columns or four stacked blocks: Status, Owner, Next action, Review date. Add a footer: “Pending is not a plan.”
