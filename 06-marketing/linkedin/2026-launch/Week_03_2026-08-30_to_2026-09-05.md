## P07 — Three problems. Three Heutrix starting points.

- Publish: Week 3 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: four-page LinkedIn document
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: confirm the three-product architecture and Page CTA. Do not add prices.

### Post copy

You should not need to diagnose your own problem before speaking with a workflow specialist.

Bring the operating tension. The right starting point depends on the decision in front of you.

**“Several workflows are messy. We do not know what to fix first.”**

Start with **Heutrix Diagnostics**: agree a focused investigation boundary, understand the current workflow and compare sensible options. Leave with a decision and action brief, including no build where that is the useful answer.

**“One important workflow is clear. It needs to work better.”**

Start with **Heutrix Workflow Transformation**, our primary implementation service: redesign, implement, test and hand over one bounded non-clinical workflow. The improvement may be a clearer process, better use of existing systems or a scoped build.

**“Staff are using AI, but our boundaries and review rules are unclear.”**

Start with **Heutrix AI Guardrails**: define approved, conditional and prohibited uses, information boundaries, accountable review and practical staff guidance.

These are independent starting points. You do not need to buy Diagnostics before Workflow Transformation or AI Guardrails.

And sometimes the right next step is no Heutrix project.

One high-level problem is enough for a 20-minute workflow fit call. No process map, technical brief or sensitive information is needed.

Talk to Heutrix.

## P08 — A workflow is not transformed when the build goes live

- Publish: Week 3 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider leaders, operations managers and workflow owners
- Format: four-page LinkedIn document
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: confirm delivery wording and avoid implying every project includes the same technology.

### Post copy

A workflow is not transformed when the build goes live.

The team still needs to test it, understand it and take ownership.

Heutrix Workflow Transformation follows four stages:

**Diagnose** — understand the current workflow, its boundary and the baseline or baseline plan. This is part of implementation; it does not require a separate Diagnostics purchase.

**Triage** — agree the future state, responsibilities, dependencies, controls and written acceptance criteria.

**Build** — implement the smallest useful improvement and test it with the people who will use it. That can mean changing the process or configuring existing systems; new software is only one option.

**Handover** — guide and train the team, transfer agreed access and documentation, and make ownership, maintenance and limitations clear.

Mapping and design happen before implementation. Testing happens before acceptance. Handover makes the improvement usable beyond the project.

The result is one bounded non-clinical workflow implemented, tested and handed over, within a written scope.

Save the four stages for the next time “go live” is treated as the finish line.

## P09 — What your team should own at handover

- Publish: Week 3 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers and workflow owners
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

If only the consultant understands the new workflow, handover has not happened.

Before a project closes, the client should know:

- who owns the workflow;
- how staff complete the normal path;
- how exceptions are handled and escalated;
- where status and next action are recorded;
- who administers the agreed system;
- which access, licence and vendor dependencies remain;
- how the workflow was tested;
- what the known limitations are; and
- what is maintenance, a defect or a future change.

The agreed accounts, access and available configuration or export artefacts should be client-controlled at handover where the written scope and third-party platform allow it.

Documentation should not be a screenshot archive. It should help the next responsible person operate and maintain the workflow without reconstructing the project from memory.

Before your next implementation, ask one question:

**What will our team be able to run, explain and maintain when the project ends?**
