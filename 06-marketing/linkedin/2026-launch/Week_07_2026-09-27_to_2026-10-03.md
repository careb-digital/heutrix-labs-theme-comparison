# Week 7 — Allied-health referral field guide

## P19 — Referral tracking field guide

- Publish: Week 7 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: allied health practices
- Format: LinkedIn document
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`

### Post copy

If referral tracking is difficult, do not begin by adding more status labels.

Begin with the decisions the practice needs to make.

A practical referral view may include:

- date received
- referral type or service requested
- completeness status
- current stage
- current owner
- next action
- next review date
- exception or reason the referral cannot progress
- final outcome and closure date

The exact fields depend on the practice, its systems and its approved information-handling rules.

The design test is simple:

Can the right person see what needs attention without reading every email or asking for an update?

Do not duplicate clinical detail into a tracker merely because the column is available. Keep the operating view limited to what is necessary for the workflow.

Save this field guide for your next referral-process review.

## P20 — A reminder is not the same as ownership

- Publish: Week 7 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: allied health practices
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

An automated reminder can tell the team that a referral follow-up or review is due.

It cannot decide who owns the next action unless the workflow already does.

Before adding reminders, clarify:

- What triggers the follow-up?
- Who is responsible?
- What happens if the person cannot be reached?
- How many attempts are appropriate under the practice's approved process?
- When is the item escalated, paused or closed?
- Where is the outcome recorded?

Without those rules, reminders can create a larger queue of notifications while the underlying responsibility stays unclear.

Use automation after the practice has defined the action and the exception path.

The reminder should support ownership, not substitute for it.

## P21 — Synthetic before-and-after: allied-health referral

- Publish: Week 7 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: allied health practices
- Format: before/after diagram
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: keep the word “synthetic” on the visual and in the post. Do not present this as a client result.

### Post copy

**Synthetic example—not a client case study.**

Imagine a small allied health practice where referrals arrive through a shared inbox.

**Before**

- Staff use flags and folders differently.
- Missing information is requested by email.
- The owner is asked for status updates.
- Referrals that cannot proceed have no consistent close.
- A weekly count requires manual reconstruction.

**Possible future state**

- The inbox remains the entry point.
- Each referral receives a controlled operating record.
- Stage, owner, next action and review date are visible.
- Missing-information and not-a-fit paths are defined.
- Restricted clinical information stays in the approved source system.
- Closure reasons support a simple, trustworthy review.

The point is not that every practice needs this exact design.

The point is that the future state answers ownership, visibility, exceptions and closure before anyone chooses a tool.

Save the four design questions: ownership, visibility, exceptions and closure. Which one would improve the referral journey most?
