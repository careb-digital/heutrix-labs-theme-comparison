# Week 13 — Demonstrate the method with clearly bounded evidence

## P37 — Synthetic walkthrough: one referral from arrival to close

- Publish: Week 13 Monday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: allied health practices
- Format: LinkedIn document
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: keep “synthetic example” visible on every page. Delivery owner approves the logic.

### Post copy

**Synthetic example—not a client workflow or clinical model.**

Follow one fictional allied-health referral from arrival to close.

At each stage, ask a different operating question.

**Arrival** — What starts the record, and how do we avoid parallel copies?

**Initial review** — What information is required to decide the next operational step?

**Waiting** — Who owns missing information, and when is it reviewed again?

**Decision** — Who can decide the operational outcome, and where is it recorded?

**Contact or booking** — What action is expected, by whom and by when?

**Exception** — What happens when the normal path cannot continue?

**Close** — What outcomes are allowed, what is communicated and what counts as done?

Notice what is not decided by the map: clinical judgement, professional obligations and practice-specific requirements remain with the appropriate qualified people.

The workflow makes responsibility and status visible around those decisions.

That is the difference between mapping tasks and designing an operating process.

#AlliedHealth #ReferralWorkflow #ProcessMapping

### Document brief

Eight pages: synthetic-example cover; arrival; review; waiting; decision/action; exception; close; final page with the seven diagnostic questions. Use fictional identifiers only and no clinical detail.

## P38 — Baseline one measure before changing a workflow

- Publish: Week 13 Wednesday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: text
- Status: `draft_owner_review`

### Post copy

Before changing a workflow, choose one measure that shows how it behaves now.

Not a grand KPI framework. One trustworthy baseline.

Depending on the workflow, that could be:

- time from arrival to first action
- number of open items older than an agreed review point
- percentage of items with a named next owner
- number of times information is requested again
- time spent preparing the weekly status update
- number of items reopened after an incomplete close

Define exactly what is included, where the data comes from and who checks it.

Then make one controlled improvement and compare.

The measure does not prove that every change was caused by the intervention. It does give the team a more disciplined basis for deciding whether the workflow is improving.

If the baseline takes days of manual clean-up, that is also useful information about the current system.

## P39 — What a useful Heutrix Diagnostics output should let you decide

- Publish: Week 13 Friday, 8:15 am Australia/Sydney
- Audience: disability-provider decision-makers first; allied-health decision-makers second
- Format: sample document pages plus text
- Status: `draft_asset_and_owner_review`
- Human check: check the sample against the agreed Diagnostics scope and the [working architecture](../../../02-market-and-offers/offers/offer-strategy-2026-09/06-integrated-offer-architecture.md). Keep proposed format/readout commitments out of the post; label the sample in both copy and artwork.

### Post copy

**Synthetic sample—not client work.**

Heutrix Diagnostics is useful when the organisation can make a better decision afterwards.

The output should help you answer:

- What is happening now?
- Where are ownership, information, waiting or exceptions breaking down?
- Which problems matter most?
- What can be simplified before technology is added?
- Which improvement options are proportionate?
- What information and access controls are needed?
- What must remain a human decision?
- What should happen next—and what should not happen yet?

The output should translate the work into a defined workflow boundary, current-state map, baseline plan, ranked bottlenecks and dependencies, feasible options, a recommended future state, draft acceptance criteria and a decision-ready action brief. An implementation scope belongs in that brief only when the evidence supports proceeding.

It is not a promise that a build will follow.

A sound diagnosis can justify a process-only change, a separate implementation scope, further specialist advice or no build.

Start with one workflow and one decision worth making clearly.
