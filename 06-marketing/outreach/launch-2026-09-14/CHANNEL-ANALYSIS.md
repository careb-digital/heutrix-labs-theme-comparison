# Which outreach suits Heutrix?

**Reviewed:** 15 September 2026. **Status:** evidence-backed recommendation for the existing launch test; channel performance is unvalidated. This analysis informs [STRATEGY.md](STRATEGY.md), which remains the operating plan. [READINESS.md](READINESS.md) remains the activation and change record.

## Recommendation

Prioritise **founder-led, permissioned conversations about one workflow**, starting with welcomed introductions and genuine professional relationships. Develop a small referral-partner network alongside those conversations. Use LinkedIn founder posts and the company Page to help buyers understand and check Heutrix's work; use one relevant resource or approved case study to support the conversation.

The practical path is:

**Recognised problem → welcomed conversation → relevant example → 20-minute fit call → suitable written scope.**

This is the best starting fit for the available evidence, not a demonstrated lowest-cost acquisition channel. Heutrix has approved delivery examples, three defined offers and a working enquiry destination, but no campaign conversion history or verified addressable founder network was supplied. If the available permissioned network is small, use public participation and welcomed introductions to build it; do not fill the batch with unsolicited messages.

## Channel comparison

Ratings below are qualitative judgements about fit, trust, buyer access, effort and measurability. They are not response-rate benchmarks. Email, LinkedIn messages and telephone are delivery methods; a warm introduction or inbound request describes how the relationship began.

| Priority | Approach | Why it suits Heutrix | Main limitation | Decision for this test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcomed founder introductions and existing relationships | Creates room to discuss the actual workflow and explain bounded proof; little setup cost | Network size and permissions are unknown; founder time limits scale | Primary direct-conversation motion, using M01/M02 and M13–M16 |
| 2 | Referral partners serving disability providers | Advisers may encounter handover and administration problems in existing relationships | Takes time to establish fit; partner enthusiasm is not provider demand | Up to two partner contacts weekly within the existing ten-contact ceiling; M05/M17 |
| 3 | Founder LinkedIn posts and genuine public discussion | Gives a buyer context for who is doing the work and a way to engage voluntarily | Audience relevance and actual reach are unknown; likes do not establish buying intent | Use the existing founder sequence and answer substantive questions; no assumed reach advantage |
| 4 | Individually researched, permissioned email or LinkedIn outreach | Can connect one observed business signal with one relevant question | Research takes time; a signal or published address does not establish campaign permission | Small supplementary cohort; choose the channel the person welcomed, using M14–M16 |
| 5 | LinkedIn company Page | Provides a consistent home for methods, offers and useful assets | Page activity alone may produce little qualified demand | Retain three prepared posts per week as credibility support; review resulting conversations |
| 6 | Provider communities and small partner-hosted sessions | Useful problem discussions can lead to volunteered enquiries | Group rules, access and relevant attendance are unverified | Contribute under actual rules. Consider one small session only after repeated interest; M19 |
| Support | Resource pages, proof pages and search visibility | Helps a buyer assess Heutrix and take the next step in their own time | Passive distribution and search may take time; downloads are not identifiable leads | Reuse existing approved assets and contact flow; no new gated funnel |
| Defer | Cold telephone campaigns or uninvited office visits | Can reach an operator directly in some situations | Intrusive, hard to contextualise and operationally unprepared for this launch | Use agreed calls only; a future cold-calling programme needs its own process and current rule check |
| Defer | Paid search, LinkedIn/Meta ads and purchased lists | Potentially broader distribution | No proven acquisition economics, targeting response or approved spend; list purchase does not establish consent | Reconsider only after the message, conversion path, capacity and acquisition-cost ceiling are evidenced |
| Defer | Facebook expansion, Instagram and mass email/DM sequences | Additional possible audiences | Adds execution load before audience fit is known; Facebook is owner-deferred | Preserve the existing reserves. Do not add channel obligations or automated outreach |

LinkedIn prohibits unauthorised scraping, messaging automation and artificial engagement. Keep this motion manual and use legitimate native publishing features where available. [LinkedIn's policy](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387), checked 15 September 2026.

## Who to approach and what to say

Research organisations through their own public websites, public business announcements and legitimate professional participation. Record the source and date in the approved lead system. A vacancy, multiple services or a new site is a conversation cue, not proof that a workflow is broken.

| Buyer role | Useful opening question | Relevant support | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner, director or general manager | Which recurring workflow needs management attention because its status or next action is hard to see? | Workflow Bottleneck Scorecard if the priority is unclear | Establish the consequence, decision owner and willingness to consider scoped paid work |
| Operations or service-delivery manager | When an enquiry is waiting to become a service, can the team see who acts next and when? | Enquiry-to-Service-Start Visibility Starter Kit | Discuss one handover and a useful observable improvement |
| Intake or administration lead | Where do you have to chase another person to find out what happens next? | Relevant resource and a high-level example | Understand daily use; involve the workflow owner with the person's agreement |
| Finance or administration manager, when a real signal supports it | When an invoice discrepancy is found, is there a clear owner and resolution path? | Approved [invoice discrepancy case](../../case-studies-and-assets/08-invoice-discrepancy-workflow.md) | Explore the exception workflow, not financial advice or a guaranteed saving |
| Allied health practice owner or manager | When a referral is waiting, can the team see what information is missing and the next review date? | General workflow resource, or clearly labelled illustrative example | Keep the discussion about non-clinical administration; use M03 |
| A buyer who raises administrative AI use | Which task is proposed, what information is involved and who reviews the result? | AI Guardrails Staff Starter Pack | Establish the governance question and suitable reviewer; do not lead every conversation with AI |

Keep intake as the opening test for the first two weeks. Use workforce or finance examples only when the prospect's actual problem supports them, and tag those conversations separately. The [worker lifecycle case](../../case-studies-and-assets/01-support-worker-lifecycle-process-map.md) demonstrates cross-team process mapping; it is not evidence of a delivered participant-intake outcome. No allied-health client outcome is established by the disability proof collection.

### Referral-partner priorities

1. **Sector accountants and bookkeepers:** possible visibility of recurring invoice, reconciliation or handover problems. Keep professional accounting advice with them.
2. **IT providers and software implementation advisers serving providers:** possible process and adoption gaps around existing systems. Confirm responsibilities and system feasibility; assume no vendor partnership.
3. **Operations, workforce and quality advisers:** possible ownership and handover issues adjacent to their work. Separate workflow delivery from audit, legal and compliance assurance.

These are partner hypotheses, not identified firms or established relationships. Use permitted existing connections first. Explain good fit, no fit and the introduction process in one conversation. A prospective provider should welcome the introduction before contact details are shared. No commission, exclusivity or referral obligation is proposed.

## Four-week test and capacity

Retain the 14 September–9 October campaign window, with 9 October as the review checkpoint. As at 15 September, actual publication and outreach are unverified. Start at the next covered slot; record the actual start and extend observation if there are fewer than four active weeks. Do not infer a missed post was published or compress missed contacts into later days.

Use the existing weekly ceiling: **20 organisations researched, up to ten permissioned first contacts, and up to two partner contacts included in that ten.** A practical mix is up to six relationship/introduction contacts, two partner contacts and two other individually researched permissioned contacts. Unused capacity is acceptable. The 80/20 disability/allied-health research allocation remains a test setting.

Suggested allocation of the existing five-hour weekly allowance:

| Work | Time |
|---|---:|
| Research and check fit, sources and permission | 75 minutes |
| Write individual messages and conduct partner follow-up | 90 minutes |
| Reuse prepared Page/founder content and participate in relevant discussion | 60 minutes |
| Triage, agreed follow-ups and weekly measurement | 75 minutes |

Fit calls and scoping require additional time. This allowance is a planning assumption, not confirmed staffing. Reduce research or posting if response coverage needs more time. Shashane and Rochelle's confirmed enquiry roles do not assign them all outreach, publishing or fit calls.

### Compare routes without overstating the result

- Record **relationship origin** separately from **delivery channel**: introduction via email is an introduction, with email as its delivery channel.
- Distinguish end-buyer replies from partner replies. A partner saying “sounds useful” is not a qualified provider opportunity. Track partner conversations, welcomed introductions and resulting qualified provider calls as separate counts.
- Count unique people for contact/reply measures and unique organisations for qualified opportunities. Deduplicate cross-channel contact and introductions; keep one agreed active conversation per organisation initially.
- Keep unsolicited inbound separate from the outbound contact denominator. Record both first-known source and volunteered supporting touchpoints, without tracking pixels or inferred browsing identity.
- Use the funnel definitions and thresholds in [STRATEGY.md](STRATEGY.md). Compare qualified provider next steps and actual time spent, not impressions alone. Small warm and researched cohorts are observational and are not a randomised channel trial.
- Do not promise a winner after four weeks. If evidence is thin, continue the best-supported route and record uncertainty. If positive replies fail to become suitable work, inspect qualification, proof relevance and the next commitment before expanding volume.

## Permission and practical boundaries

ACMA requires consent, sender identification, contact details and a functional unsubscribe route for commercial electronic messages; it recognises express and inferred consent and prohibits an electronic marketing message merely to ask for consent. Heutrix's campaign uses a recorded, reviewed permission basis; a public address, accepted connection or resource download alone is insufficient. Keep the stop route working for at least 30 days; honour requests immediately operationally, within ACMA's five-working-day limit. [ACMA guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam), checked 15 September 2026.

When there is no contact permission, use useful public content, permitted community participation, an in-person conversation or a welcomed introduction. A partner cannot bypass these boundaries by sending unsolicited marketing on Heutrix's behalf. Use M13 only during an appropriate existing conversation; permission to send one resource does not establish permission for a marketing sequence.

Use high-level business context, with one question or one clear next action. Discuss scope and fees privately. Contact records, consent evidence, suppression and client information belong outside Git in the approved system. Confirm current operational readiness before activating the campaign; writing this strategy does not close those existing gates.
