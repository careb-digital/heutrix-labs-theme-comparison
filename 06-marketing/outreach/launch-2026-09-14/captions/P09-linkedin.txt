If only the consultant understands the new workflow, handover has not happened.

Before a project closes, the client should know:

- who owns the workflow;
- how staff complete the normal path;
- how exceptions are handled and escalated;
- where status and next action are recorded;
- who administers the agreed system;
- which access, licence and vendor dependencies remain;
- how the workflow was tested;
- what the known limitations are; and
- what is maintenance, a defect or a future change.

The agreed accounts, access and available configuration or export artefacts should be client-controlled at handover where the written scope and third-party platform allow it.

Documentation should not be a screenshot archive. It should help the next responsible person operate and maintain the workflow without reconstructing the project from memory.

Before your next implementation, ask one question:

What will our team be able to run, explain and maintain when the project ends?
